## U.S. inflation, income, and output

- The Bureau of Economic Analysis reported July personal income up 0.4% month over month, disposable personal income up 0.5%, current-dollar consumer spending up 0.2%, and a 3.0% personal saving rate.
- The July PCE price index rose 0.2% month over month and 3.7% year over year. Core PCE prices rose 0.2% month over month and 3.3% year over year. The 3.7% headline rate was unchanged from June; the FactSet economist consensus reported by the Associated Press was 3.6%.
- BEA's second estimate put second-quarter real GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate, unchanged from the advance estimate and below the first quarter's 2.1%. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 4.2%, and real gross domestic income rose 2.2%.
- Second-quarter PCE prices rose at a 5.3% annual rate and core PCE prices rose 3.6%. Profits from current production increased by $400.9 billion.

## Labor, manufacturing, and housing

- July nonfarm payroll employment declined by 23,000 and the unemployment rate was 4.1%. May and June payroll changes were revised down by a combined 103,000. The Bureau of Labor Statistics described both July payroll employment and unemployment as little changed.
- July durable-goods orders rose 1.1% month over month to $339.3 billion. Orders excluding transportation rose 0.4%, orders excluding defense rose 1.3%, and transportation-equipment orders rose 2.3%. Census states that the survey does not have computable sampling errors.
- July new single-family home sales fell 10.5% to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 607,000 and were 6.3% below a year earlier. Inventory was 488,000 homes, equal to 9.6 months of supply; the median sales price was $393,800.

## Policy and energy

- The Federal Open Market Committee maintained a 3.50%–3.75% federal-funds target range on July 29 by a 9–3 vote. Three members preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The next scheduled FOMC meeting is September 15–16 and includes economic projections.
- EIA data for the week ended August 21 showed commercial crude inventories up 0.1 million barrels to 428.9 million, 1% above the five-year seasonal average.
- Gasoline inventories fell 2.5 million barrels and were 6% below the five-year average; distillate inventories fell 2.2 million barrels and were about 14% below the five-year average.
- Four-week total petroleum products supplied averaged 20.5 million barrels per day, down 3.0% from a year earlier.
