## Rates and monetary policy

### U.S. Treasury curve

Treasury par yields for August 19 were 3.77% at 1 month, 3.86% at 3 months, 4.19% at 2 years, 4.35% at 5 years, 4.65% at 10 years, and 5.19% at 30 years. On August 18, the corresponding 2-, 5-, 10-, and 30-year values were 4.19%, 4.37%, 4.71%, and 5.28%.

The Treasury Department said it planned at least to double longer-term Treasury purchases over September 9–November 4 to support liquidity in longer-dated nominal sectors. Contemporaneous reporting put the 10-year yield at 4.64% and the 30-year yield at 5.18% after the announcement, versus 4.71% and 5.28% late August 18. Intraday market levels and official daily par yields use different observation conventions.

### Federal Reserve

The July 29 FOMC statement kept the federal-funds target range at 3.50%–3.75%. The vote was 9–3; three participants preferred a 25-basis-point increase. Minutes released August 19 recorded that most participants expected inflation to step down over time, while many described a risk that inflation could remain more persistent. The minutes also recorded upside-skewed inflation risks, solid economic activity, stable labor-market conditions, and investment strength concentrated in artificial-intelligence-related spending. These are minutes of the July meeting, not a current policy decision.

The next scheduled FOMC meeting is September 15–16, inside the monthly horizon and outside the weekly horizon.

## International macro and policy

The ECB held its three key rates unchanged on July 23: 2.25% for the deposit facility, 2.40% for main refinancing operations, and 2.65% for the marginal lending facility. Its next policy meeting is scheduled for September 9–10.

Eurostat's preliminary estimate put second-quarter euro-area GDP at +0.4% quarter over quarter and +1.0% year over year, and EU GDP at +0.5% quarter over quarter and +1.2% year over year. The estimate was preliminary and based on incomplete member-state data. Eurostat's July flash estimate put euro-area inflation at 2.9% year over year after 2.8% in June, with energy at +10.0% and services at +3.3%. These are flash estimates.

China's National Bureau of Statistics reported on August 17 that industrial value added rose 5.3% year over year in January–July and 4.5% in July. The July manufacturing purchasing managers' index was 49.2 and the services production index was 49.3. January–July retail sales of goods and services rose 2.6%; consumer-goods retail sales rose 1.2% in January–July and 0.6% in July. Fixed-asset investment fell 6.7% in January–July; real-estate development investment fell 19.2% and manufacturing investment fell 1.7%. January–July goods trade rose 17.3% in yuan terms, with exports up 14.0% and imports up 22.0%; July goods trade rose 19.2%. The surveyed urban unemployment rate was 5.2% in July. The agency noted a supply-demand imbalance and publishes detailed coverage and methods notes with the release.

China's July CPI was -0.1% month over month and +0.5% year over year; core CPI was +0.3% month over month and +0.9% year over year. In August 19 Asian trading, contemporaneous reporting put Japan's Nikkei 225 down 3.2% and South Korea's Kospi down 5.8%.

The Bank of Japan's next scheduled monetary-policy meeting is September 17–18.
