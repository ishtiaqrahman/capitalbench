## Source ledger

### 1. U.S. services activity

- Source: Institute for Supply Management, *September 2026 ISM Services PMI Report*.
- URL: https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/services/september/
- Released: 2026-10-05 at 10:00 ET; ISM's release roundup was published 2026-10-06.
- Reported facts: the Services PMI was 54.9, down 0.5 point from August and above the 50 expansion threshold for a 27th consecutive month. Business activity was 56.5, new orders 59.8, employment 50.1, and supplier deliveries 53.2.
- Price and flow detail: the Prices Index increased 1.4 points to 74.0, its highest reading since July 2022. Backlogs increased to 56.6, while new export orders fell 9.4 points to 46.9.
- Uncertainty: diffusion indexes measure the breadth and direction of survey responses, not the magnitude of sector output. Supplier deliveries is inverted, so a reading above 50 denotes slower deliveries.

### 2. U.S. international trade

- Source: U.S. Census Bureau and U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, *U.S. International Trade in Goods and Services, August 2026*.
- URL: https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/us-international-trade-goods-and-services-august-2026
- Released: 2026-10-06 at 08:30 ET.
- Reported facts: the goods-and-services deficit was $105.6 billion in August, up $12.7 billion from a revised $92.8 billion in July. Exports increased $4.5 billion, or 1.4%, to $315.2 billion; imports increased $17.2 billion, or 4.3%, to $420.8 billion.
- Detail: the goods deficit increased $12.8 billion to $136.6 billion and the services surplus increased by less than $0.1 billion to $31.0 billion. In real 2017-dollar terms, the goods deficit increased 8.2% to $114.7 billion.
- Revisions and uncertainty: July goods imports were revised up $4.4 billion. The headline values are seasonally adjusted but not adjusted for price changes and remain subject to later revisions.

### 3. U.S. energy outlook

- Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration, *Short-Term Energy Outlook, October 2026*.
- URLs: https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/index.php and https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/global_oil.php
- Released: 2026-10-06; forecast inputs completed 2026-10-01.
- Reported forecast: EIA raised its fourth-quarter 2026 Brent crude forecast to $105 per barrel, $14 above the September forecast. It forecast full-year Brent averages of $96 in 2026 and $84 in 2027.
- Market assumptions: EIA estimated September Middle East crude-production shut-ins at 4.8 million barrels per day, down from 5.8 million in August, and forecast fourth-quarter shut-ins averaging 4.5 million barrels per day. It estimated global inventories fell 1.9 million barrels per day in the third quarter and forecast a further 0.7 million-barrel-per-day draw in the fourth quarter.
- Product detail: September U.S. gasoline and diesel prices averaged $4.35 and $6.29 per gallon. East Coast distillate inventories were 32% below their five-year seasonal average in September.
- Uncertainty: this is a conditional forecast. EIA explicitly stated that inputs were finalized October 1 and therefore did not specifically incorporate later events, including the G7 announcement of October 2; it also expected greater short-term oil-price volatility than the forecast path displays.

### 4. U.S. labor market

- Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, *The Employment Situation — September 2026*.
- URL: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm
- Released: 2026-10-02 at 08:30 ET.
- Reported facts: nonfarm payroll employment increased by 29,000 in September and the unemployment rate was 4.2%. Labor-force participation was 61.8%, the employment-population ratio 59.2%, average hourly earnings rose 0.1% over the month and 3.0% over twelve months, and the average workweek was 34.4 hours.
- Revisions: July payroll growth was revised from +21,000 to -10,000 and August from +162,000 to +133,000, a combined downward revision of 60,000.
- Uncertainty: September establishment data are preliminary. BLS states that an over-the-month establishment-survey change of roughly 122,000 is needed for statistical significance; survey estimates are subject to sampling error and later revision.

### 5. U.S. manufacturing

- Source: Institute for Supply Management, *September 2026 ISM Manufacturing PMI Report*.
- URL: https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/september/
- Released: 2026-10-01.
- Reported facts: the Manufacturing PMI was 54.5, 0.1 point below August and above the 50 expansion threshold. Production was 56.7, new orders 55.3, order backlogs 56.4, and employment 52.7.

- Sources: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, *Personal Income and Outlays, August 2026* and *Gross Domestic Product, Second Quarter 2026 — Third Estimate*.
- URLs: https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026 and https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product
- Released: 2026-09-30.
- Reported facts: personal income increased 0.2% in August, disposable personal income 0.3%, nominal PCE 0.9%, and real PCE 0.6%. The saving rate was 4.1%. The PCE price index increased 0.3% over the month and 3.4% over twelve months; excluding food and energy, it increased 0.2% and 3.0%.
- Growth detail: real GDP increased at a 2.2% annual rate in the second quarter after revised 2.5% growth in the first quarter.
- Uncertainty: the releases incorporated the annual National Economic Accounts update, including historical revisions beginning in January 2021. Quarterly GDP remains subject to annual revisions.

### 7. Federal Reserve policy and projections

- Sources: Federal Reserve Board, September 16 FOMC statement and Summary of Economic Projections.
- URLs: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm and https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm
- Released: 2026-09-16.
- Reported facts: the FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal-funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00%. The statement characterized activity as expanding at a solid pace and inflation as elevated.
- Projection detail: median 2026 projections were 2.3% real GDP growth, 4.1% unemployment, 3.7% PCE inflation, 3.4% core PCE inflation, and a 4.1% year-end federal-funds rate. Seventeen of eighteen participants judged risks to PCE inflation to be weighted to the upside.
- Uncertainty: projections are participants' conditional assessments, not a committee commitment. Minutes of the September meeting were scheduled for October 7, and the next FOMC meeting for October 27-28.
