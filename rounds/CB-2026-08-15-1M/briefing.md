## Market close and cross-asset levels

The Russell 2000 rose 0.5% to 3,068.42. For the week, the S&P 500 rose 0.4%, the Nasdaq rose 0.1%, the Russell 2000 rose 1.1%, and the Dow fell 0.6%. The S&P 500 had set an all-time high on August 13.

Through August 14, the reported year-to-date changes were +13.7% for the S&P 500, +15.0% for the Nasdaq, +23.6% for the Russell 2000, and +11.8% for the Dow. Brent crude rose 1.7% on August 14 to $88.52 per barrel.

The U.S. Treasury's August 14 par yield curve was 3.86% at three months, 3.98% at one year, 4.17% at two years, 4.36% at five years, 4.68% at ten years, and 5.25% at both twenty and thirty years. Treasury describes these as interpolated par yields derived from indicative bid-side quotations rather than transaction prices.

## Inflation and monetary policy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that headline CPI rose 0.1% in July after falling 0.4% in June. The 12-month headline rate was 3.4%. CPI excluding food and energy rose 0.2% in July and 2.5% over 12 months. Shelter rose 0.1% and accounted for about two-thirds of the monthly headline increase. Food rose 0.1% and energy fell 1.5%. Over 12 months, energy rose 14.7% and gasoline rose 24.6%.

The Producer Price Index for final demand was unchanged in July after a revised 0.1% decline in June. Final demand rose 4.7% over 12 months. Final-demand services rose 0.2%, construction rose 2.2%, and goods fell 0.7% in July. Within goods, energy fell 3.1%, foods fell 0.9%, and goods excluding foods and energy rose 0.1%. Final demand excluding foods, energy, and trade services rose 0.4% for the month and 4.7% over 12 months.

On July 29, the Federal Open Market Committee maintained the federal-funds target range at 3.50%–3.75% by a 9–3 vote. The committee statement described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace, inflation as elevated, and uncertainty about the outlook as elevated. Three voters preferred a 25-basis-point increase.

## Demand, inventories, and production

The Census Bureau estimated July retail and food-services sales at $763.6 billion, down 0.6% from June with a reported ±0.4 percentage-point sampling margin and up 5.0% from July 2025 with a ±0.5-point margin. Sales excluding motor vehicles and parts fell 0.3%; sales excluding motor vehicles, parts, and gasoline stations fell 0.2%. Motor-vehicle and parts dealers fell 1.8%, nonstore retailers fell 2.2%, clothing and accessories stores rose 1.9%, and food services and drinking places rose 0.5%.

June combined manufacturing and trade sales were $2.1113 trillion, down 1.1% from May and up 10.0% from a year earlier. June inventories were $2.7402 trillion, virtually unchanged from May and 3.0% above a year earlier. The inventory-to-sales ratio was 1.30, compared with 1.39 a year earlier.

The Federal Reserve reported that industrial production rose 0.1% in June and at a 4.0% annual rate in the second quarter. Manufacturing output was unchanged in June. Capacity utilization was 76.1%, 3.3 percentage points below its 1972–2025 average.

The Census Bureau estimated June housing starts at a 1.427 million annual rate, 19.0% above May with a reported ±15.9% confidence interval. Single-family starts were 895,000, 0.2% below May with a ±10.2% interval. Building permits were at a 1.367 million annual rate, down 3.0% from May and 2.3% from a year earlier.

## Labor market

BLS reported a July nonfarm payroll change of -23,000 and an unemployment rate of 4.1%. May payroll growth was revised from +129,000 to +63,000 and June from +57,000 to +20,000, reducing the combined two-month estimate by 103,000. The labor-force participation rate was 61.4%, the employment-population ratio was 58.9%, and average hourly earnings were 3.2% higher than a year earlier.

Local-government education employment fell by 50,000, retail trade by 19,000, and financial activities by 14,000; health-care employment rose by 22,000. For June, BLS reported 7.4 million job openings, 5.3 million hires, 5.4 million total separations, 3.2 million quits, and 1.8 million layoffs and discharges. The May openings estimate was revised down by 57,000 to 7.5 million.

## Output, income, and business surveys

The Bureau of Economic Analysis advance estimate put second-quarter real GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate, compared with 2.1% in the first quarter. Consumer spending, investment, and exports increased; government spending decreased and imports increased. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers increased at a 3.9% annual rate, compared with 1.7% in the first quarter.

BEA reported June personal-income growth of 0.2%, current-dollar consumer-spending growth of 0.3%, real consumer-spending growth of 0.4%, and a personal saving rate of 2.7%. The June PCE price index fell 0.1% for the month and rose 3.7% over 12 months. Core PCE rose 0.1% for the month and 3.3% over 12 months.

ISM reported a July Manufacturing PMI of 55.6, its seventh consecutive expansion reading. New orders were 56.7, production 58.5, employment 52.8, supplier deliveries 58.9, customers' inventories 40.7, and prices 71.1. ISM reported a July Services PMI of 54.1, its 25th consecutive expansion reading. Business activity was 59.1, new orders 57.2, employment 47.4, supplier deliveries 52.8, and prices 70.3. ISM indexes are diffusion measures of reported direction and breadth rather than dollar output.
