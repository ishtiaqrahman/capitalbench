## July 14 Market Close And Breadth

- The S&P 500 rose 28.25 points, or 0.4%, to 7,543.59; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 9.63 points, or less than 0.1%, to 52,508.27; the Nasdaq Composite rose 233.83 points, or 0.9%, to 26,107.01; and the Russell 2000 rose 11.60 points, or 0.4%, to 2,964.76.
- Year to date through July 14, the S&P 500 was up 10.2%, the Dow was up 9.2%, the Nasdaq was up 12.3%, and the Russell 2000 was up 19.5%.
- Micron Technology rose 4.9% and Nvidia rose 4.1% after falling 4.4% and 3.5%, respectively, on July 13.
- Goldman Sachs rose 9.0%, Citigroup fell 5.3%, and IBM fell 25.2%. Associated Press reported IBM's decline was its worst day since at least 1972, according to FactSet.
- Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.7% and Shanghai rose 1.4%. Associated Press reported China's June exports rose 27% from a year earlier.

## Inflation, Rates, And Federal Reserve Facts

- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported the all-items CPI fell 0.4% in June after rising 0.5% in May. The 12-month rate slowed to 3.5% from 4.2%.
- Core CPI was unchanged in June after rising 0.2% in May. The 12-month core rate slowed to 2.6% from 2.9%.
- The energy index fell 5.7% in June but was 15.7% above a year earlier. Gasoline fell 9.7% in June and was 26.7% above a year earlier.
- The food index rose 0.2% in June and 3.0% over 12 months. Shelter rose 0.1% in June, its smallest monthly change since January 2021, and 3.3% over 12 months.
- The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.58% from 4.62% late July 13 and remained above 3.97% before the Iran war began.
- Associated Press reported CME Group data showed a less-than-17% implied probability of a rate increase at the next meeting after the CPI release, down from nearly 42% on July 13.
- Chairman Kevin Warsh said the June FOMC held the federal-funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. He described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace and the labor market as broadly stable, and said the Committee had no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation.
- Associated Press reported Warsh offered no signal on the Federal Reserve's next policy step during his July 14 House testimony.

## Labor And Corporate Facts

- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported June payroll growth of 57,000, an unemployment rate of 4.2%, a labor-force participation rate of 61.5%, and 3.5% year-over-year average hourly earnings growth.
- April and May payroll growth were revised down by a combined 74,000. June employment rose in professional and business services, social assistance, and health care, while leisure and hospitality employment fell by 61,000.
- Associated Press reported five large U.S. banks posted record profits and results above analyst expectations. JPMorgan reported $16.9 billion of Q2 profit, 35% year-over-year markets-revenue growth, and 86% growth in equity-markets revenue.
- Bank of America reported consumer spending increased; JPMorgan reported consumer-banking revenue of $20.3 billion, up 8% from a year earlier; and Wells Fargo reported higher consumer spending with lower charge-offs and delinquencies.
- TSMC reported unaudited June revenue of NT$442.680 billion, up 67.9% from a year earlier, and first-half revenue of NT$2,404.484 billion, up 35.6%.
- Tesla reported Q2 production of 451,758 vehicles, deliveries of 480,126 vehicles, and energy-storage deployments of 13.5 GWh. Tesla said these measures should not be relied on as indicators of quarterly financial results.

## Energy And Strait Of Hormuz Status

- Brent crude settled at $84.73 a barrel, up $1.43 or 1.7%, and WTI settled at $79.34, up $1.20 or 1.5%. Both closed at their highest levels since mid-June.
- Reuters reported U.S. diesel futures were up about 21% in July through July 14, compared with about 14% for U.S. crude.
- The U.S. military said it reimposed its blockade of Iranian ports on July 14 after renewed attacks on commercial ships. The United States also carried out another wave of strikes.
- President Donald Trump dropped the previously announced 20% transit fee before the blockade resumed, citing requests from Gulf allies.
- Associated Press reported that before the war, one-fifth of traded crude oil and natural gas passed through the strait. The interim agreement's permanent-war-ending and nuclear talks had stalled at the cutoff.
- Control of the strait, shipping volumes, military duration, energy flows, and further diplomatic or military actions remained unresolved at the cutoff.
