## Economic activity and demand

- The Bureau of Economic Analysis advance estimate put second-quarter real GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate, after 2.1% in the first quarter. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers increased at a 3.9% annual rate.
- BEA reported that second-quarter consumer spending, investment, and exports increased; government spending decreased and imports increased. The GDP second estimate and corporate-profits release is scheduled for August 26.
- In June, BEA reported personal income and disposable personal income up 0.2%, current-dollar consumption up 0.3%, real consumption up 0.4%, and a 2.7% personal saving rate.
- The Census Bureau estimated June retail and food-services sales at $768.6 billion, up 0.2% from May with a ±0.4-point sampling interval and up 6.7% from a year earlier with a ±0.5-point interval.

## Prices, productivity, and labor costs

- BEA's second-quarter gross-domestic-purchases price index increased at a 5.7% annual rate. The PCE price index increased at 5.1%, and the PCE price index excluding food and energy increased at 3.4%.
- In June, the PCE price index decreased 0.1% from May and increased 3.7% from a year earlier. Excluding food and energy, it increased 0.1% from May and 3.3% from a year earlier.
- BLS preliminary data showed second-quarter nonfarm-business productivity up at a 1.4% annual rate and unit labor costs up 1.3%. From a year earlier, productivity rose 2.2% and unit labor costs rose 1.4%.
- BLS reported second-quarter real hourly compensation down at a 3.1% annual rate and labor share at 52.9%, the lowest reading in the series beginning in 1947. Revised figures are scheduled for September 3.

## Labor demand and survey employment

- BLS reported June job openings at 7.4 million, hires at 5.3 million, total separations at 5.4 million, quits at 3.2 million, and layoffs and discharges at 1.8 million; each was described as unchanged or little changed.
- May JOLTS openings were revised down by 57,000, hires up by 82,000, and total separations up by 159,000.
- The July ISM manufacturing employment index was 52.8, while the services employment index was 47.4. The July Employment Situation was scheduled for August 7 at 08:30 EDT and was unreleased at the cutoff.

## Monetary policy and market rates

- On July 29, the Federal Reserve maintained the federal-funds target range at 3.50%-3.75% by a 9-3 vote. Three voters preferred a 25-basis-point increase.
- The Federal Reserve described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace, inflation as elevated relative to 2%, and uncertainty as elevated partly because of the Middle East conflict.
- On August 6, Treasury's nominal par yields were 3.99% at six months, 4.06% at one year, 4.25% at two years, 4.40% at five years, 4.69% at ten years, and 5.22% at thirty years.
- On August 6, Treasury's real par yields were 2.17% at five years, 2.43% at ten years, and 2.98% at thirty years.

## Global policy and cross-asset facts
