## Newly released activity and labor indicators

- The Institute for Supply Management reported an August Manufacturing PMI of 54.6%, down from 55.6% in July and above 50 for an eighth consecutive month.
- ISM's August subindexes were: New Orders 53.7%, Production 58.3%, Employment 51.2%, Supplier Deliveries 59.3%, Inventories 50.6%, Prices 71.1%, Backlog of Orders 51.8%, New Export Orders 53.2%, and Imports 52.5%. Survey diffusion indexes measure the share and direction of reported change rather than measured output levels.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 7.271 million job openings in July, with a 4.4% openings rate. Hires and total separations were each approximately 5.1 million. Quits were 3.1 million and layoffs and discharges were 1.7 million. BLS described those aggregate measures as little changed.
- BLS revised June job openings down by 177,000 to 7.2 million. June hires and total separations were each revised to 5.3 million.
- The Census Bureau reported July construction spending at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of $2,157.6 billion, down 0.5% from June. The headline release is subject to revision and survey error.

## Growth, consumption, industry, and housing

- BEA's second estimate showed second-quarter real GDP increasing at a 1.5% annual rate after 2.1% in the first quarter. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers increased 4.2%, real gross domestic income increased 2.2%, and the average of real GDP and real GDI increased 1.8%.
- BEA reported that the second-quarter gross-domestic-purchases price index increased at a 5.8% annual rate. The PCE price index increased 5.3%, and PCE prices excluding food and energy increased 3.6%.
- July current-dollar personal income increased 0.4%, disposable personal income increased 0.5%, and current-dollar personal consumption expenditures increased 0.2%. Real PCE changed by less than 0.1%. The personal saving rate was 3.0%.
- Advance July retail and food-services sales were $763.6 billion, down 0.6% from June and up 5.0% from July 2025. Census reported margins of error of plus or minus 0.4 percentage point for the monthly change and plus or minus 0.5 percentage point for the annual change. The figures were not adjusted for price changes.
- Industrial production and manufacturing production each increased 0.2% in July. Manufacturing production excluding motor vehicles and parts increased 0.4%. Manufacturing capacity utilization was 76.0%, 2.2 percentage points below its 1972–2025 average.
- Advance durable-goods orders increased 1.1% to $339.3 billion in July. Orders excluding transportation increased 0.4%, and orders excluding defense increased 1.3%.
- July housing starts were at a 1.239 million annual rate, down 12.4% from June with a plus or minus 9.5% margin. Building permits were at a 1.443 million rate, up 5.0%.
- New single-family home sales were at a 607,000 annual rate, down 10.5% from June with a plus or minus 14.0% margin. New-home inventory represented 9.6 months of supply, and the median sales price was $393,800. The reported monthly sales change was not statistically distinguishable from zero at the stated confidence level.

## Inflation and monetary policy

- U.S. CPI increased 0.1% in July and 3.4% from a year earlier. CPI excluding food and energy increased 0.2% monthly and 2.5% over twelve months. Energy prices declined 1.5% during July but were 14.7% above July 2025.
- The July PCE price index increased 0.2% monthly and 3.7% over twelve months. Excluding food and energy, PCE prices increased 0.2% monthly and 3.3% over twelve months.
- The Producer Price Index for final demand was unchanged in July and increased 4.7% over twelve months. Final-demand services increased 0.2%, while final-demand goods declined 0.7%.
- On July 29, the FOMC maintained the federal-funds target range at 3.50%–3.75%. Nine voting members supported the decision; three members dissented in favor of a 25-basis-point increase. The next scheduled FOMC meeting is September 15–16.
- Associated Press reported that CME FedWatch implied a 66% probability of a 25-basis-point increase at the September FOMC meeting as of September 1. This was a changing market-derived probability, not Federal Reserve guidance.
- On July 23, the European Central Bank held the deposit-facility rate at 2.25%, the main-refinancing rate at 2.40%, and the marginal-lending rate at 2.65%. The ECB stated that future decisions would remain data dependent and meeting by meeting.
- The Bank of Japan held its policy rate at 1.0% on July 31 after raising it from 0.75% in June. Its July outlook described moderate growth at a decelerated rate and future policy adjustment as conditional on activity, prices, and financial conditions.
