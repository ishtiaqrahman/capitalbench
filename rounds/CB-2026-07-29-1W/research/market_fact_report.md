## Rates, Policy, and Inflation

- The Federal Open Market Committee voted 9-3 on July 29 to maintain the federal-funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie Logan preferred to raise the range by 25 basis points.
- The FOMC said available indicators suggested solid economic activity, job gains had kept pace with population growth, unemployment had changed little, and inflation remained elevated relative to 2%. It also said uncertainty about the outlook remained elevated, in part because of the Middle East conflict, and that supply shocks including energy were adding to inflation.
- AP reported that, before the FOMC decision, futures pricing implied about a 34% probability of a rate increase. This was market-implied pricing sourced by AP to CME Group, not a Federal Reserve forecast.
- AP reported that the two-year U.S. Treasury yield ended July 29 at 4.24%, down from 4.26% late July 28, while the ten-year yield rose to 4.68% from 4.61%.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on July 14 that June CPI fell 0.4% month over month and rose 3.5% over twelve months. Core CPI was unchanged on the month and rose 2.6% over twelve months. Energy fell 5.7% in June and rose 15.7% over twelve months. BLS notes that recent chained-CPI readings remain subject to revision.
- BLS reported on July 15 that June final-demand PPI fell 0.3% month over month and rose 5.5% over twelve months. Final-demand goods fell 1.4%, services rose 0.2%, and final demand excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.1% on the month and 5.1% over twelve months. PPI estimates can be revised.
- The Bureau of Economic Analysis reported that the May PCE price index rose 0.4% month over month and 4.1% over twelve months; core PCE rose 0.3% and 3.4%, respectively. Real PCE rose 0.3% and the personal saving rate was 3.0%. BEA scheduled June Personal Income and Outlays and the advance estimate of second-quarter GDP for July 30 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Those outcomes were unavailable by the cutoff.

## Labor, Consumption, Manufacturing, and Housing

- BLS reported on July 2 that June nonfarm payroll employment increased by 57,000 and unemployment was 4.2%. Labor-force participation fell 0.3 percentage point to 61.5%. April and May payroll estimates were revised down by a combined 74,000. These survey estimates are subject to sampling error and later revision.
- AP reported on July 28 that the Conference Board consumer-confidence index fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June. Responses were collected from July 1 through July 22. References to jobs and unemployment increased, views of the current labor market weakened, and six-month labor-market expectations improved slightly but remained negative.
- The Census Bureau reported on July 16 that June retail and food-services sales were $768.6 billion, up 0.2% from May and 6.7% from June 2025. The monthly estimate carried a ±0.4 percentage-point sampling margin, so Census did not establish that the monthly change differed from zero.
- Census reported on July 27 that June durable-goods orders rose 0.3% to $334.8 billion after a 4.0% May decline. Orders excluding transportation rose 0.6%; orders excluding defense rose 0.3%; and computers and electronic-product orders rose 3.1% to $31.1 billion.
- Census and HUD reported on July 24 that June new-home sales ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 628,000, 1.6% above May and 5.6% below June 2025. The stated sampling margins were ±14.8% and ±13.2%, respectively, so neither comparison was statistically conclusive. Inventory represented 9.3 months of supply and the median sales price was $398,300.

## Company Results Available at the Cutoff

- Microsoft reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $90.0 billion, up 18% year over year, operating income of $40.6 billion, up 18%, and GAAP diluted EPS of $4.81, up 32%. Microsoft Cloud revenue was $59.3 billion, up 27%; Azure and other cloud-services revenue grew 43%; commercial remaining performance obligation was $678 billion, up 84%.
- Microsoft reported More Personal Computing revenue of $12.9 billion, down 4%; Windows OEM and devices revenue declined 7%; and Xbox content and services revenue declined 10%. Its GAAP results included a $3.2 billion gain on the Anthropic investment, lower value-realignment-program costs, severance, and an Xbox content-asset impairment. Constant-currency and non-GAAP figures are company-defined measures.
- Meta reported second-quarter revenue of $60.801 billion, up 28% year over year, while costs and expenses rose 55% to $42.026 billion. Operating income fell 8% to $18.775 billion, net income fell 14% to $15.848 billion, and diluted EPS fell 13% to $6.18.
- Meta reported daily active people of 3.60 billion, up 3%, ad impressions up 14%, and average price per ad up 12%. Results included $2.40 billion of legal charges and $1.18 billion of severance charges; capital expenditures including finance-lease principal payments were $31.08 billion and free cash flow was $784 million.
- Meta forecast third-quarter revenue of $61 billion to $64 billion, full-year 2026 expenses of $165 billion to $169 billion, and full-year capital expenditures of $130 billion to $145 billion. These are company forecasts, not observed outcomes. Meta also said legal and regulatory matters could significantly affect results and that some youth-related trials presented potential material losses.

## Cross-Asset and International Context

- AP reported that October Brent crude settled July 29 at $88.09 per barrel, up 7.3%. AP reported that Brent had traded near $72 early in July and above $102 in the prior week amid the Middle East conflict and uncertainty over tanker access through the Strait of Hormuz.
- AP reported that South Korea's Kospi fell 6.0% on July 29 after a 10.8% decline on July 28; SK Hynix fell 9.6%. The Kospi remained up 34.4% for 2026 through July 29. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 2.0% and Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.5%.
- The European Central Bank kept its policy rates unchanged on July 23: 2.25% on the deposit facility, 2.40% on main refinancing operations, and 2.65% on the marginal lending facility. The ECB said energy-price uncertainty remained high, the full inflation effect of the energy shock had yet to appear, and decisions would remain meeting-by-meeting without a precommitted path.
- Eurostat reported final June euro-area inflation of 2.8%, down from 3.2% in May. Energy contributed 0.77 percentage point and services 1.51 percentage points to the annual rate. Eurostat scheduled its July flash estimate for July 31.
- China's National Bureau of Statistics reported on July 15 that real GDP increased 4.3% year over year in the second quarter and 0.9% from the first quarter; first-half GDP increased 4.7% from a year earlier. In the first half, manufacturing value added rose 5.6%, high-technology manufacturing rose 13.3%, fixed-asset investment fell 5.7%, and real-estate development investment fell 18.0%.
- The Bank of Japan lists a monetary-policy meeting for July 30-31, with its policy communication and Outlook Report on July 31. No outcome was available at the cutoff.
