## Rates, Inflation, and Policy

- The FOMC voted 9-3 to maintain the federal-funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. Three members preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The FOMC described activity as solid, unemployment as little changed, inflation as elevated relative to 2%, and outlook uncertainty as elevated in part because of the Middle East conflict.
- Before the FOMC decision, futures pricing implied about a 34% probability of an increase. This was market-implied pricing, not a Federal Reserve forecast.
- The two-year U.S. Treasury yield ended July 29 at 4.24%, down from 4.26%, while the ten-year yield rose to 4.68% from 4.61%.
- June CPI fell 0.4% from May and rose 3.5% over twelve months. Core CPI was unchanged on the month and rose 2.6% over twelve months. Energy fell 5.7% on the month.
- June final-demand PPI fell 0.3% from May and rose 5.5% over twelve months. Goods prices fell 1.4% while services prices rose 0.2%.
- May PCE inflation was 0.4% month over month and 4.1% over twelve months; core PCE was 0.3% and 3.4%, respectively. June PCE and advance second-quarter GDP are scheduled for July 30; outcomes were unavailable at the cutoff.

## Economy, Companies, and Cross-Assets

- June payrolls increased by 57,000, unemployment was 4.2%, and labor-force participation was 61.5%. April and May payroll estimates were revised down by a combined 74,000.
- July consumer confidence was 90.8, down from 92.2 in June. Current labor-market views weakened while six-month labor-market expectations improved slightly but remained negative.
- June retail and food-services sales rose 0.2% from May and 6.7% over twelve months. The monthly estimate's ±0.4 percentage-point margin did not establish a statistically significant monthly change.
- June durable-goods orders rose 0.3%; orders excluding transportation rose 0.6%; and computers and electronic-product orders rose 3.1%.
- June new-home sales ran at a 628,000 annual rate with 9.3 months of supply. The reported monthly and annual changes had sampling margins wider than the estimates.
- Microsoft reported quarterly revenue of $90.0 billion, up 18%; Microsoft Cloud revenue of $59.3 billion, up 27%; Azure revenue growth of 43%; and commercial remaining performance obligation of $678 billion, up 84%. More Personal Computing revenue fell 4%, Windows OEM and devices revenue fell 7%, and Xbox content and services revenue fell 10%.
- Meta reported revenue of $60.801 billion, up 28%, while costs and expenses rose 55%; operating income fell 8% and net income fell 14%. Meta forecast third-quarter revenue of $61 billion to $64 billion and full-year capital expenditures of $130 billion to $145 billion. These are company forecasts, not observed outcomes.
- October Brent crude settled at $88.09 per barrel, up 7.3%. Strait of Hormuz tanker access and the Middle East conflict remained unresolved.
- South Korea's Kospi fell 6.0% after a 10.8% decline the previous day; Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 2.0% and Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.5%.
- The ECB kept its three policy rates unchanged on July 23. June euro-area inflation was 2.8%. China reported second-quarter real GDP growth of 4.3% year over year and 0.9% quarter over quarter.
