## Labor, Claims, And Income Facts

- BLS reported that June nonfarm payroll employment increased by 57,000 and the unemployment rate was 4.2 percent.
- BLS reported 7.1 million unemployed people in June.
- Labor force participation decreased by 0.3 percentage point to 61.5 percent, and the employment-population ratio was 59.0 percent.
- The long-term unemployed count was 1.9 million and accounted for 27.3 percent of unemployed people.
- Total private payrolls increased by 49,000 in June; professional and business services increased by 36,000, health care and social assistance increased by 46,600, and leisure and hospitality decreased by 61,000.
- Average hourly earnings for all private employees increased 0.3 percent to $37.64 and were up 3.5 percent from a year earlier.
- The average workweek was unchanged at 34.3 hours.
- BLS revised April and May payrolls down by a combined 74,000.
- DOL reported initial claims of 215,000 for the week ending June 27, down 1,000 from the revised previous week.
- DOL reported the four-week initial-claims average at 222,000, down 2,500 from the revised previous average.
- DOL reported insured unemployment at 1,814,000 for the week ending June 20, up 2,000 from the revised previous week, with an insured unemployment rate of 1.2 percent.
- ADP reported private-sector employment increased by 98,000 in June and annual pay was up 4.4 percent.
- BEA reported May personal income increased 0.7 percent, disposable personal income increased 0.7 percent, current-dollar PCE increased 0.7 percent, and real PCE increased 0.3 percent.
- BEA reported the May PCE price index increased 0.4 percent month over month and 4.1 percent year over year; core PCE increased 0.3 percent month over month and 3.4 percent year over year.
