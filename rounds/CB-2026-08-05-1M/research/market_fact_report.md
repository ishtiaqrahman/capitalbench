## Monetary policy, financing, growth, inflation, and labor

- The FOMC held the federal-funds target at 3.50%-3.75% on July 29 by a 9-3 vote. Three members preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The statement characterized activity as expanding at a solid pace and inflation as elevated relative to the 2% goal.
- Treasury announced an August refunding package of $125 billion: $58 billion of three-year notes, $42 billion of ten-year notes, and $25 billion of thirty-year bonds. The securities are scheduled to be auctioned August 11, 12, and 13 and to settle August 17. Treasury expects nominal coupon and floating-rate note auction sizes to remain unchanged for at least the next several quarters.
- Treasury said the refunding would retire about $96.3 billion of privately held securities and raise about $28.7 billion of new cash. Its assumptions included a $950 billion end-September cash balance and a possible late-October cash-balance peak of about $1.05 trillion, plus or minus $50 billion.
- BEA's advance Q2 estimate showed real GDP increasing at a 1.5% annual rate. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 3.9%. The gross-domestic-purchases price index increased 5.7% annualized; headline and core PCE prices increased at 5.1% and 3.4% annual rates.
- For June, personal income and disposable personal income each increased 0.2%, nominal consumer spending increased 0.3%, real spending increased 0.4%, and the saving rate was 2.7%. Headline PCE prices fell 0.1% from May and rose 3.7% from a year earlier; core PCE rose 0.1% monthly and 3.3% yearly.
- June CPI fell 0.4% from May and was 3.5% above June 2025. CPI excluding food and energy was unchanged on the month and 2.6% higher over the year. July CPI is scheduled before the weekly August 12 close.
- June final-demand PPI declined 0.3% on the month and was 5.5% higher over twelve months. Goods prices fell 1.4% while services prices rose 0.2%. July PPI is scheduled for August 13, after the weekly close and inside the monthly window.
- BLS reported 57,000 additional payroll jobs in June and a 4.2% unemployment rate, describing both as little changed. The July Employment Situation is scheduled for August 7.
- June job openings were little changed at 7.4 million and hires were unchanged at 5.3 million. Total separations were 5.4 million, including 3.2 million quits and 1.8 million layoffs and discharges.
- Axios reported that ADP estimated 44,000 private jobs added in July, down from 95,000 in June and the smallest gain since January. It also reported year-over-year pay growth of 4.4% for job stayers and 7.0% for job changers. This private-payroll estimate is separate from the forthcoming BLS report.

## Business activity, consumers, housing, and trade

- ISM's July Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.6 from 53.3, the seventh consecutive expansion reading and the highest since May 2022. New orders were 56.7 and production 58.5; all five components used in the headline PMI were in expansion territory.
- ISM's July Services PMI was 54.1 versus 54.0 in June, the twenty-fifth month of expansion. Business activity was 59.1 and new orders 57.2. Employment fell to 47.4, while the prices index rose to 70.3; prices exceeded 70 in four of the last five months.
- June industrial production increased 0.1% and was 1.1% above a year earlier. Manufacturing output was unchanged, and capacity utilization held at 76.1%, 3.3 percentage points below its 1972-2025 average. June values are preliminary.
- Advance June retail and food-services sales were $768.6 billion, 0.2% above May and 6.7% above a year earlier. Census said the 90% confidence interval for the monthly change included zero, and the series is not adjusted for prices.
- June housing starts were at a 1.427 million annual rate, 19.0% above revised May; single-family starts were 895,000, 0.2% below May. Permits were at a 1.367 million rate, down 3.0%. Census reported wide confidence intervals.
- July Consumer Confidence declined 1.4 points to 90.8. The Present Situation Index fell to 114.9 and the Expectations Index was unchanged at 74.7. The preliminary online survey ended July 22, before the latest renewed fighting.
- The June goods-and-services trade deficit narrowed $4.4 billion to $73.3 billion. Exports fell $2.9 billion to $314.7 billion and imports fell $7.3 billion to $388.0 billion. For the first half of 2026, the deficit was 33.8% below the same 2025 period; exports were up 11.7% and imports up 0.4%.

## Global, energy, and geopolitical facts

- The IMF's July update projected global growth of 3.0% in 2026 and 3.4% in 2027. It said global disinflation had stalled and identified renewed conflict and financial-market repricing as downside risks. These figures are forecasts.
- The ECB left its deposit, main-refinancing, and marginal-lending rates at 2.25%, 2.40%, and 2.65% on July 23. It said the full inflation effect of the energy shock had not yet played out and did not pre-commit to a rate path.
- The IEA reported June global oil supply rebounding by 4.1 million barrels per day to 98.8 million, still 9.4 million below prewar levels. Gulf oil exports including bypass routes reached 16.1 million barrels per day, versus a 24 million prewar average. The IEA stated that its outlook depends on de-escalation and continued recovery in transit volumes.
- On August 5, AP reported that Iranian and Omani negotiators had completed a draft arrangement concerning Strait of Hormuz routes but were awaiting final Iranian approval. AP also reported disagreement over port-blockade relief, Iranian control, and possible service fees. The waterway's status was therefore unresolved at the cutoff.
- AP reported that roughly one fifth of globally traded oil and natural gas passed through the strait before the war. This is background attributed to AP, not a contemporaneous flow measurement.
