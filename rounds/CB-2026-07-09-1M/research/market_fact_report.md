## Market Close And Cross-Asset Regime

- On Thursday, July 9, the S&P 500 rose 60.93 points, or 0.8%, to 7,543.64; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 139.02 points, or 0.3%, to 52,487.41; and the Nasdaq Composite rose 336.24 points, or 1.3%, to 26,206.89.
- A July 9 delayed market snapshot showed the Russell 2000 at 2,992.54, up 36.15 points or 1.22%, and VIX at 15.84, down 1.06 points or 6.27%.
- Brent crude fell 2.2% to $76.30 a barrel from $78.02 on July 8. It remained above its $71.80 level at the end of the prior week.
- The 10-year Treasury yield ended near 4.54%, down from 4.56% late July 8. A separate July 9 mortgage report placed it at 4.55% at midday, versus 4.49% one week earlier and 3.97% before the Iran war began.
- AAA data cited by AP put the average U.S. price of regular gasoline at $3.85 per gallon, up five cents overnight and 68 cents from one year earlier.
- AP reported Micron Technology rose 4.5% after an update on its central New York semiconductor site and cited surging demand for memory in the AI era.
- AP reported PepsiCo fell 3.3% after results showed weaker trends in its North American food and drink businesses.
- Internationally, AP reported South Korea's Kospi up 0.6% after a 5.3% decline the prior day, Shanghai up 1.7%, Paris up 0.9%, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng down 0.7%.

## Labor, Inflation, And Monetary Policy

- Initial claims for unemployment benefits were 215,000 in the week ended July 4, down 2,000 from the prior week and below the 220,000 FactSet consensus cited by AP.
- The four-week average of initial claims fell 3,750 to 218,750. Continuing claims for the week ended June 27 rose 8,000 to 1.81 million.
- The latest June employment report showed nonfarm payroll growth of 57,000 and an unemployment rate of 4.2%. AP noted that the unemployment-rate decline from 4.3% partly reflected people leaving the labor force.
- The June FOMC minutes said total PCE inflation was 3.8% and core PCE inflation was 3.3% in April. Staff estimated May total PCE inflation at 4.1% and core PCE inflation at 3.4%.
- The Federal Reserve maintained the federal funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75% by a 12-0 vote at the June meeting. The minutes described inflation as elevated and noted energy supply shocks, tariff pass-through, and AI-related demand among the factors affecting prices.
- The minutes said the median Desk survey path implied no change in the target range through the start of 2027 and one rate cut in the second quarter of 2027. Market pricing suggested one rate increase for mid-2027, though the minutes said term premiums likely affected that measure.

## Business Activity And Supply Conditions

- ISM reported the June Manufacturing PMI at 53.3, down 0.7 point from May and consistent with a sixth consecutive month of manufacturing expansion.
- Manufacturing new orders were 56.0, production 52.2, employment 49.7, supplier deliveries 57.4, and prices 73.0. The prices index fell 9.1 points from May but remained in increasing territory.
- ISM reported the June Services PMI at 54.0, down 0.5 point from May and consistent with a 24th consecutive month of services expansion.
- The services prices index was 67.7, down 3.6 points from May and below 70 for the first time since February. ISM said all items listed in short supply in June were inputs needed for data-center construction.

## Housing And Consumer Datapoints

- June existing-home sales fell 2.4% from May to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 4.09 million, below the approximately 4.21 million FactSet consensus. Sales were 2.8% above June 2025.
- The June median existing-home sale price rose 1.8% from one year earlier to $440,600, the highest level in National Association of Realtors data beginning in 1999.
- Unsold existing-home inventory was 1.56 million at month-end, down 0.6% from May and up 1.3% from one year earlier. That represented 4.6 months of supply. First-time buyers accounted for 33% of purchases.
- Freddie Mac reported the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 6.49%, up from 6.43% one week earlier and below 6.72% one year earlier. The 15-year rate was 5.82%, up from 5.79% one week earlier and below 5.86% one year earlier.
- PepsiCo reported Q2 net revenue rose 6.4% to $24.2 billion, above the $23.9 billion FactSet consensus. North American snack volume was flat and beverage volume fell 4%; overall snack volume rose 3% and beverage volume rose 2% with stronger overseas sales.
- PepsiCo reported Q2 net income of $2.98 billion and adjusted earnings of $2.20 per share, one cent above the FactSet consensus. Management said higher gasoline prices weighed on North American consumers and described the future path as dependent on gasoline prices.

## International, Technology, And Geopolitical Datapoints

- China passenger-car exports rose 80% from one year earlier in June to about 905,000, while domestic passenger-car sales fell 26% to about 1.5 million.
- China passenger-vehicle exports rose 72% in the first half to more than 4.4 million. Domestic passenger-car sales were nearly 8.3 million in the first half.
- S&P Global Ratings forecast cited by AP placed full-year 2026 Chinese passenger-car export growth at 30% to 50%. AlixPartners forecast about 10 million Chinese vehicle exports in 2026, up from around 7 million in 2025; both are source forecasts, not observed results.
- The U.S. military said it struck 90 targets in Iran on July 9. Iran responded with attacks directed at U.S.-allied countries in the region, and AP described the exchange as threatening the interim ceasefire arrangement.
- AP reported preliminary Lloyd's List Intelligence data showing at least 576 ships transited the Strait of Hormuz in June, versus 233 in May and more than 3,100 in June 2025.

## Source-Reported Uncertainty And Caveats

- AP reported that the July 9 exchange of attacks threatened the interim Iran ceasefire arrangement, while the market recap also reported that oil prices eased that day. The duration, scope, and market effects of the conflict remained uncertain at the cutoff.
- The FOMC minutes recorded multiple inflation paths and noted uncertainty from energy, tariffs, AI demand, and Middle East developments. Desk survey and market-implied policy paths are expectations, not policy commitments.
- Weekly jobless claims are volatile. The four-week average fell, while continuing claims rose in the latest observations.
- ISM diffusion indexes remained in expansion, while manufacturing employment was below 50 and both manufacturing and services price indexes remained in increasing territory.
- Housing data showed slower monthly sales alongside a record national median price, increased year-over-year inventory, and substantial regional variation.
- TSMC states that its financial calendar is subject to change. Company and government release dates should be treated as scheduled statuses, not completed events.
