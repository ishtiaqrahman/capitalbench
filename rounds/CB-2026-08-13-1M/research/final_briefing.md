## Market close and cross-asset levels

The Associated Press reported that the S&P 500 gained 0.3% on August 12 to 7,748.50, its first gain since setting an all-time high on August 7. The Nasdaq Composite gained 0.5% to 26,588.49, the Russell 2000 gained 0.6% to 3,045.48, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell less than 0.1% to 53,770.27. Through August 12, the AP reported year-to-date gains of 13.2% for the S&P 500, 14.4% for the Nasdaq, 22.7% for the Russell 2000, and 11.9% for the Dow.

The U.S. Treasury's August 12 constant-maturity curve was 3.87% at three months, 4.00% at one year, 4.20% at two years, 4.38% at five years, 4.68% at ten years, and 5.24% at both twenty and thirty years. The two-, five-, and ten-year yields were respectively 2, 1, and 2 basis points below their August 11 levels; the thirty-year yield was unchanged. Treasury describes these as interpolated par yields from indicative bid-side quotations.

The Associated Press reported Brent crude at $88.98 a barrel on August 12, up 0.1% for the session after moving between gains and losses. The AP reported continued uncertainty involving the Iran conflict and crude flows.

## Inflation and monetary policy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that headline CPI rose 0.1% in July after falling 0.4% in June. The 12-month headline rate was 3.4%, down from 3.5%. CPI excluding food and energy rose 0.2% in July and 2.5% over 12 months, down from a 2.6% annual rate in June.

Shelter rose 0.1% and accounted for roughly two-thirds of the monthly headline increase. Food rose 0.1%; food at home fell 0.1%; food away from home rose 0.3%. Energy fell 1.5% in July and gasoline fell 2.9%, while the 12-month changes remained +14.7% for energy and +24.6% for gasoline. Airline fares rose 2.2% in July and 25.5% over 12 months.

On July 29, the Federal Open Market Committee maintained the federal-funds target range at 3.50%–3.75% by a 9–3 vote. The committee statement described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace and inflation as elevated relative to the 2% goal. Three voters preferred a 25-basis-point increase.

## Labor market

BLS reported a July nonfarm payroll change of -23,000 and an unemployment rate of 4.1%, describing both as little changed. May payroll growth was revised from +129,000 to +63,000 and June growth from +57,000 to +20,000, reducing the combined two-month estimate by 103,000.

The July labor-force participation rate was 61.4%, 0.7 percentage point below January. Average hourly earnings were 3.2% higher than a year earlier. Local-government education employment fell by 50,000, retail trade by 19,000, and financial activities by 14,000; health care employment rose by 22,000.

For June, BLS reported 7.4 million job openings and a 4.4% openings rate. Hires were 5.3 million, total separations 5.4 million, quits 3.2 million, and layoffs and discharges 1.8 million. The May openings estimate was revised down by 57,000 to 7.5 million.

## Growth, demand, and production

The Bureau of Economic Analysis advance estimate put second-quarter real GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate, compared with 2.1% in the first quarter. Consumer spending, investment, and exports increased; government spending decreased and imports increased. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose at a 3.9% annual rate, compared with 1.7% in the first quarter.

BEA reported June personal-income growth of 0.2%, current-dollar consumer-spending growth of 0.3%, real consumer-spending growth of 0.4%, and a personal saving rate of 2.7%. The June PCE price index fell 0.1% for the month and rose 3.7% over 12 months. Core PCE rose 0.1% for the month and 3.3% over 12 months.

The Federal Reserve reported that industrial production rose 0.1% in June and at a 4.0% annual rate in the second quarter. Manufacturing output was unchanged in June. Capacity utilization was 76.1%, 3.3 percentage points below its 1972–2025 average.

The Census Bureau estimated June housing starts at a 1.427 million annual rate, 19.0% above May with a reported ±15.9% confidence interval. Single-family starts were 895,000, 0.2% below May with a ±10.2% interval. Building permits were at a 1.367 million annual rate, down 3.0% from May and 2.3% from a year earlier.

## Business surveys

The Institute for Supply Management reported a July Manufacturing PMI of 55.6, up from 53.3 and the highest reading since May 2022. New orders were 56.7, production 58.5, employment 52.8, supplier deliveries 58.9, customers' inventories 40.7, and prices 71.1. ISM classified manufacturing as expanding, supplier deliveries as slowing, and customers' inventories as too low.

ISM reported a July Services PMI of 54.1, its 25th consecutive expansion reading. Business activity was 59.1 and new orders 57.2. Services employment was 47.4, in contraction, and the services prices index was 70.3. ISM reported expansion in 13 services industries and contraction in four.

ISM indexes are diffusion measures based on survey responses and indicate direction and breadth rather than dollar output levels.
