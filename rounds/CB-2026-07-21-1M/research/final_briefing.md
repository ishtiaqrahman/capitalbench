## U.S. economy

- BLS reported June CPI down 0.4% month over month and up 3.5% over 12 months. CPI excluding food and energy was unchanged for the month and up 2.6% over 12 months. Energy fell 5.7% in June and rose 15.7% over 12 months.
- BLS reported June final-demand PPI down 0.3% for the month and up 5.5% over 12 months. Final demand less food, energy, and trade services rose 0.1% for the month and 5.1% over 12 months.
- June nonfarm payroll employment increased by 57,000, the unemployment rate was 4.2%, and average hourly earnings rose 3.5% over 12 months. April and May payroll gains were revised down by a combined 74,000.
- Advance June retail and food-services sales rose 0.2% from May and 6.7% from June 2025. The monthly estimate carried a plus-or-minus 0.4 percentage-point sampling interval and was not adjusted for price changes.
- June industrial production rose 0.1% for the month and 1.1% over 12 months. Manufacturing was unchanged for the month, mining rose 0.4%, and total capacity utilization was 76.1%.
- Third-estimate first-quarter real GDP rose at a 2.1% annual rate. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 1.7%, real GDI rose 1.2%, and the quarterly PCE price index rose at a 4.6% annual rate.
- In May, personal income and consumer spending each rose 0.7%, the personal saving rate was 3.0%, and the PCE price index was 4.1% above its year-earlier level.
- June housing starts were at a 1.427 million annual rate, up 19.0% from revised May with a plus-or-minus 15.9 percentage-point interval. Building permits fell 3.0%. Existing-home sales fell 2.4% to a 4.09 million annual rate, and pending-home sales fell 5.4%.
- Preliminary July University of Michigan consumer sentiment was 54.4, compared with 49.5 in June and 61.7 in July 2025. Final July results are scheduled for July 31.

## Policy and published forecasts

- On June 17, the Federal Reserve maintained the federal-funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75% by a 12–0 vote.
- Federal Reserve participant medians for 2026 were 2.2% real GDP growth, 4.3% unemployment, 3.6% PCE inflation, 3.3% core PCE inflation, and a 3.8% year-end federal-funds rate. These are participant forecasts under individual appropriate-policy assumptions, not a Committee plan. The submitted ranges were 1.8%–2.6%, 4.3%–4.6%, 2.7%–4.1%, 2.6%–3.5%, and 3.4%–4.4%, respectively.
- The ECB raised its deposit, main-refinancing, and marginal-lending rates by 25 basis points to 2.25%, 2.40%, and 2.65%, effective June 17. ECB staff forecast 2026 headline inflation at 3.0% and inflation excluding energy and food at 2.5%; the ECB said it was not pre-committing to a rate path.
- The Bank of England maintained Bank Rate at 3.75% by a 7–2 vote; two members preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The Bank said the scale, duration, and propagation of the energy shock remained uncertain.
- The Bank of Japan website listed its policy rate at 1.0% on July 21.
- The IMF forecast global growth of 3.0% in 2026 and 3.4% in 2027. Its 2026 forecasts included 2.3% for the United States, 0.9% for the euro area, 1.1% for Japan, 4.6% for China, and 6.4% for India. The IMF stated that global disinflation had stalled and identified renewed conflict and financial-market repricing as downside risks.
