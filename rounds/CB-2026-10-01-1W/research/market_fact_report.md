## U.S. growth, inflation, and consumption

- **BEA, Personal Income and Outlays for August 2026; released September 30, 2026; August observation.** Personal income rose 0.2% month over month, disposable personal income rose 0.3%, nominal personal consumption expenditures rose 0.9%, and real PCE rose 0.6%. The headline PCE price index rose 0.3% month over month and 3.4% year over year; the index excluding food and energy rose 0.2% month over month and 3.0% year over year. The personal saving rate was 4.1%. BEA stated that this release incorporated the annual National Economic Accounts update, including revisions beginning with January 2021. Source: https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026
- **BEA, third estimate of second-quarter 2026 GDP; released September 30, 2026; April–June observation.** Real GDP increased at a 2.2% annualized rate, versus 1.5% in the second estimate; first-quarter growth was revised to 2.5%. BEA identified consumer spending, investment, and exports as positive contributors, while imports increased and subtract from GDP accounting. The annual update means these estimates supersede earlier vintages. Source: https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product

## Labor market

- **BLS, Employment Situation for August 2026; released September 4, 2026; August observation.** Nonfarm payrolls increased by 162,000 and unemployment was unchanged at 4.1%. Labor-force participation edged up to 61.6%. Average hourly earnings rose 0.3% month over month and 3.1% year over year. BLS revised the combined June and July payroll change upward by 55,000. The establishment and household surveys have different concepts and sampling uncertainty, and monthly estimates remain subject to revision. Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm

## Monetary policy and rates

- **Federal Reserve, FOMC statement; released September 16, 2026.** The Committee voted 12–0 to raise the federal-funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00% and continued its ample-reserves implementation framework. Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm
- **Federal Reserve, September 2026 Summary of Economic Projections; released September 16, 2026.** Median 2026 projections were 2.3% real-GDP growth, 4.1% unemployment, 3.7% PCE inflation, 3.4% core PCE inflation, and a 4.1% year-end federal-funds rate. These are individual participants' projections under their own appropriate-policy assumptions, not a Committee commitment, and the published ranges were wide. Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm
- **U.S. Treasury, Daily Treasury Par Yield Curve Rates; September 30, 2026 close.** The 2-year, 10-year, and 30-year par yields were 4.88%, 5.29%, and 5.64%, respectively. Treasury states that these are market quotations obtained around 3:30 p.m. each business day and fitted to its official curve methodology. Source: https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026

## International macro and policy

- **China National Bureau of Statistics, September PMI; released September 30, 2026.** The official manufacturing PMI rose 0.3 point to 50.1, the production index rose to 51.7, and new orders were 50.5. Large-enterprise PMI was 50.6; medium and small enterprises remained below 50 at 49.7 and 48.9. Diffusion indexes above 50 denote expansion, but survey measures are not direct output levels. Source: https://www.stats.gov.cn/zwfwck/sjfb/202609/t20260930_1965449.html
- **European Central Bank, monetary-policy decision; released September 10, 2026.** The ECB raised all three key rates by 25 basis points, taking the deposit facility to 2.50%, the main refinancing rate to 2.65%, and the marginal lending rate to 2.90% from September 16. ECB staff projected headline inflation of 3.0% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2027, and real growth of 0.9% in 2026 and 1.4% in 2027. The ECB described the outlook as highly uncertain, with upside inflation risk and downside growth risk linked in part to the Middle East energy shock. Source: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp260910~314e508016.en.html
