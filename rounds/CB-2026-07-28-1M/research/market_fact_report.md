## Labor, Consumption, Manufacturing, and Housing

- BLS reported on July 2 that June nonfarm payroll employment increased by 57,000 and unemployment was 4.2%. Labor-force participation was 61.5%. These survey estimates are subject to sampling error and later revision; the July employment report is scheduled for August 7.
- The Conference Board reported on July 28 that its consumer-confidence index fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June. AP reported that the survey collected responses from July 1 through July 22, that views of the current labor market weakened, and that six-month labor-market expectations improved slightly but remained negative.
- The Census Bureau reported on July 16 that June retail and food-services sales were $768.6 billion, up 0.2% from May and 6.7% from June 2025. The monthly estimate carried a ±0.4 percentage-point sampling margin, so Census did not establish that the monthly change differed from zero.
- Census reported on July 27 that June durable-goods orders rose 0.3% to $334.8 billion after a 4.0% May decline. Orders excluding transportation rose 0.6%; orders excluding defense rose 0.3%; and computers and electronic-product orders rose 3.1% to $31.1 billion.
- Census and HUD reported on July 24 that June new-home sales ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 628,000, 1.6% above May and 5.6% below June 2025. The stated sampling margins were ±14.8% and ±13.2%, respectively, so neither comparison was statistically conclusive. Inventory represented 9.3 months of supply and the median sales price was $398,300.

## Cross-Asset and International Context

- AP reported that October Brent crude settled July 28 at $82.08 per barrel, down 4.4%. AP reported that the September delivery contract had briefly exceeded $102 late in the prior week. Negotiations over tanker access through the Strait of Hormuz and the U.S.-Iran conflict remained unresolved.
- AP reported that South Korea's Kospi fell 10.8% on July 28 and that trading was temporarily halted during the session. The report linked the decline to large losses in SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics and concerns about developments in Chinese chipmaking equipment.
- The European Central Bank kept its policy rates unchanged on July 23: 2.25% on the deposit facility, 2.40% on main refinancing operations, and 2.65% on the marginal lending facility. The ECB said energy-price uncertainty remained high and that the full inflation effect of the energy shock had yet to appear.
- Eurostat reported final June euro-area inflation of 2.8%, down from 3.2% in May. Energy contributed 0.77 percentage point and services 1.51 percentage points to the annual rate. Eurostat scheduled its July flash estimate for July 31.
- China's National Bureau of Statistics reported on July 15 that real GDP increased 4.3% year over year in the second quarter and 0.9% from the first quarter; first-half GDP increased 4.7% from a year earlier.
- The Bank of Japan lists a monetary-policy meeting for July 30-31, with its policy communication and Outlook Report on July 31. No outcome was available at the cutoff.
