## Macro, Labor, Inflation, And Policy

- BLS reported June nonfarm payroll employment increased by 57,000. The unemployment rate was 4.2%, little changed.
- BLS reported the labor force participation rate decreased to 61.5%, and the employment-population ratio was 59.0%.
- BLS reported average hourly earnings rose 0.3% month over month in June to $37.64 and were up 3.5% over 12 months. The average workweek was 34.3 hours.
- BLS revised April payroll growth down from 179,000 to 148,000 and May down from 172,000 to 129,000, a combined downward revision of 74,000.
- BEA reported May current-dollar personal income up 0.7%, current-dollar disposable personal income up 0.7%, current-dollar PCE up 0.7%, and real PCE up 0.3%.
- BEA reported the May PCE price index up 0.4% month over month and core PCE up 0.3% month over month. The FOMC minutes said staff estimated May PCE inflation at 4.1% and core PCE inflation at 3.4%.
- On June 17, the Federal Reserve held the federal funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75% by a 12-0 vote.
- The July 8 FOMC minutes said all participants supported maintaining the target range at the June meeting. The minutes described inflation as elevated and upside price risks as elevated.
- The FOMC minutes said a few participants saw a case for raising the policy rate but supported holding the range, while most participants described scenarios in which inflation would fall over coming quarters. The minutes also said most participants pointed to scenarios where inflation could remain elevated because of AI-related demand, Middle East developments, tariffs, or other factors.
- Federal Reserve G.19 data for May showed total consumer credit about unchanged on a seasonally adjusted annual-rate basis. Revolving credit decreased at a 4.7% annual rate, nonrevolving credit increased at a 1.6% annual rate, and total outstanding consumer credit was $5,154.5 billion.

## Business Activity And Supply Chains

- ISM reported the June Manufacturing PMI at 53.3, down 0.7 point from May. ISM said the reading indicated a sixth consecutive month of manufacturing expansion.
- ISM reported manufacturing new orders at 56.0, production at 52.2, employment at 49.7, supplier deliveries at 57.4, and prices at 73.0.
- ISM manufacturing comments included references to war involving Iran, tariff and pricing volatility, AI-related capacity consumption, and shortages in semiconductors and hot rolled steel.
- ISM reported the June Services PMI at 54.0, down 0.5 point from May and consistent with a 24th consecutive month of services expansion.
- ISM reported services business activity at 55.4, new orders at 55.1, employment at 51.2, supplier deliveries at 54.4, and prices at 67.7.
- ISM services reported employment expanded for the first time in four months, while the prices index was still above 60 for a 19th consecutive month. Reported shortage categories included computers, electronic components, memory components, software licensing, steel, switchgear, transformers, and wire/cable.
