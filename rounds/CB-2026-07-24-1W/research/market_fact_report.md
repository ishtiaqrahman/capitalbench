## Prompt 1 findings

### U.S. market close and breadth

- The Associated Press reported that the S&P 500 rose 3.68 points, less than 0.1%, to 7,411.98 on July 24. The Dow rose 235.60 points, or 0.5%, to 51,947.25; the Nasdaq fell 161.87 points, or 0.6%, to 24,975.82; and the Russell 2000 fell 10.16 points, or 0.3%, to 2,930.00.
- For the week ended July 24, AP reported the S&P 500 down 0.6%, the Dow down 0.4%, the Nasdaq down 2.1%, and the Russell 2000 down 1.1%. Year to date, the same indexes were up 8.3%, 8.1%, 7.5%, and 18.1%, respectively.
- AP reported that more S&P 500 constituents rose than fell on July 24 even though large technology losses held down the capitalization-weighted indexes. Micron fell 7.0% and Broadcom fell 2.7%.
- On July 23, AP reported the S&P 500 down 1.2%, the Dow down 1.0%, and the Nasdaq down 2.2%. Alphabet fell 7.1% despite reporting stronger profit and revenue than expected; AP reported that quarterly AI investment nearly doubled from a year earlier to almost $45 billion while cloud revenue grew 82%. Tesla fell 14.5% after reporting weaker-than-expected profit.
- Reuters reported on July 24 that more than 80 S&P 500 companies had reported and aggregate second-quarter earnings were tracking 26.5% above a year earlier as of July 22, based on LSEG IBES data. This was a source-reported estimate, not a final count.

### Energy, rates, and cross-asset observations

- AP reported Brent crude settled at $96.78 per barrel on July 24, down 3.9% on the day after settling at $100.69 on July 23. It had risen during each of the first four sessions of the week and traded near $72 before the late-February Iran war.
- AP reported that attacks on two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea contributed to the July 23 oil move. AP also reported that the Strait of Hormuz remained disrupted; normally about one-fifth of globally traded oil and natural gas passes through it.
- The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield ended July 24 at 4.68%, down from 4.71% late July 23, according to AP. AP reported the yield at 3.97% before the Iran war began.
- AP reported a U.S. national average regular gasoline price of $4.10 per gallon on July 24, compared with $4.09 on July 23 and roughly $3.93 a month earlier. These figures were attributed to AAA.
- Reuters reported that federal-funds futures late July 24 priced a 38% probability of a 25-basis-point increase at the July 28-29 FOMC meeting and expected no change as the central case. These are market-implied probabilities that can change.
- AP reported that a fresh U.S. tariff round announced July 24 affected nearly all U.S. imports. AP noted that tariffs are paid by U.S. importers, which may pass costs to consumers. The implementation and pass-through remain uncertain.

### U.S. macro releases available by the cutoff

- Census and HUD estimated June new single-family home sales at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 628,000, up 1.6% from May and down 5.6% from June 2025. The stated 90% confidence intervals were ±14.8 percentage points for the monthly change and ±13.2 points for the annual change; neither change was statistically distinguishable from zero at that confidence level.
- Census estimated 485,000 new homes for sale at the end of June and 9.3 months of supply. The June median sale price was $398,300, down 3.3% from May and 2.7% from a year earlier, with wide source-reported confidence intervals.
- BLS reported June CPI down 0.4% seasonally adjusted and up 3.5% over 12 months. Core CPI was unchanged in June and up 2.6% over 12 months. The June energy index fell 5.7% in the month but was 15.7% above a year earlier.
- BLS reported June final-demand PPI down 0.3% in the month and up 5.5% over 12 months. Final-demand goods fell 1.4%, final-demand services rose 0.2%, and final demand excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.1% in June and 5.1% over 12 months.
- BLS reported June nonfarm payrolls up 57,000 and unemployment at 4.2%. April and May payroll growth was revised down by a combined 74,000. Labor-force participation fell 0.3 percentage point to 61.5%. Average hourly earnings rose 0.3% in June and 3.5% over 12 months.
- The Labor Department reported 187,000 seasonally adjusted initial unemployment claims for the week ended July 18, down 22,000 from the prior revised week. The four-week average was 207,500. Continued claims for the week ended July 11 were just under 1.8 million.
- Census estimated June retail and food-services sales at $768.6 billion, up 0.2% from May with a ±0.4-percentage-point confidence interval and up 6.7% from June 2025 with a ±0.5-point interval. April-through-June sales were 6.4% above the same 2025 period.
- The Federal Reserve estimated June industrial production rose 0.08% after a 0.14% May increase. Its current estimate put second-quarter industrial production growth at a 4.05% annual rate, subject to later revision.

### Monetary policy and international data

- At its June 16-17 meeting, the FOMC unanimously maintained the federal-funds target range at 3.50%-3.75%. Its statement said economic activity was expanding at a solid pace, inflation remained elevated relative to 2%, and energy supply shocks had raised some prices.
- On July 23, the ECB left its deposit, main refinancing, and marginal lending rates unchanged at 2.25%, 2.40%, and 2.65%. The ECB said uncertainty around the energy shock remained high and it was not pre-committing to a rate path.
- Eurostat reported euro-area annual inflation at 2.8% in June, down from 3.2% in May. The July flash estimate was scheduled for July 31.
- China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported first-half 2026 GDP up 4.7% from a year earlier at constant prices. The official quarterly data put second-quarter growth at 4.3% year over year after 5.0% in the first quarter.
- China’s statistics agency reported first-half per-capita disposable income up 5.2% nominally and 4.2% after price adjustment. It reported June industrial producer prices up 4.1% from a year earlier and down 0.3% from May.
- The Bank of Japan scheduled a two-day monetary-policy meeting for July 30-31 with an Outlook Report view released after the meeting.
