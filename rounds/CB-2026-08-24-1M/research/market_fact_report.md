## Monetary policy, inflation, and rates

- On July 29, the Federal Open Market Committee maintained the federal funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. The vote was 9–3; three members preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The committee described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace and inflation as elevated in part because of energy and supply disruptions.
- The next scheduled FOMC meeting is September 15–16 and includes a Summary of Economic Projections.
- The July Consumer Price Index rose 0.1% from June and 3.4% from July 2025. Core CPI rose 0.2% month over month and 2.5% year over year. Energy prices fell 1.5% in July but were 14.7% above a year earlier.
- The July Producer Price Index for final demand was unchanged from June and was 4.7% above July 2025. Final-demand services rose 0.2%, construction rose 2.2%, and goods fell 0.7%.
- In June, the PCE price index fell 0.1% from May and rose 3.7% from a year earlier. Core PCE rose 0.1% month over month and 3.3% year over year.

Sources: [Federal Reserve July 29 statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm); [Federal Reserve meeting calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm?categoryId=1&orient=1); [BLS July CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.htm); [BLS July PPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.htm); [BEA June personal income and outlays](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-june-2026).

## Growth, spending, labor, and housing

- BEA's advance estimate put second-quarter real GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate after 2.1% in the first quarter. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose at a 3.9% annual rate after 1.7% in the first quarter.
- June real personal consumption expenditures rose 0.4% from May. Current-dollar personal income and disposable personal income each rose 0.2%, and the personal saving rate was 2.7%.
- July nonfarm payroll employment fell by 23,000. The unemployment rate was 4.1%, the labor-force participation rate was 61.4%, and temporary layoffs rose by 153,000 to 921,000.
- Advance July retail and food-services sales were $763.6 billion, down 0.6% from June and up 5.0% from July 2025. These estimates are not adjusted for price changes.
- July privately owned housing starts were at a 1.239 million seasonally adjusted annual rate, down 12.4% from June and 13.5% from July 2025. Building permits were at a 1.443 million rate, up 5.0% from June and 3.1% from a year earlier.

Sources: [BEA second-quarter GDP advance estimate](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-2nd-quarter-2026); [BEA June personal income and outlays](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-june-2026); [BLS July employment situation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm); [Census July retail sales](https://www.census.gov/retail/sales.html); [Census July residential construction](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/current/).
