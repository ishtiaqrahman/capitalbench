Goods-producing employment fell 10,000, including a 17,000 manufacturing decline; service employment rose 48,000, led by 45,000 in education and health services. ADP revised July job growth to 46,000 from 44,000.
- ADP's August pay data showed median base pay up 3.2% from a year earlier for all workers, 3.0% for job stayers, and 4.7% for job changers. Median gross pay rose 4.7% overall, 4.4% for stayers, and 7.3% for changers. ADP said its employment measure draws on payroll data covering more than 26 million private-sector employees; it is independent of the forthcoming Bureau of Labor Statistics payroll report.
- The Labor Department reported September 3 that initial unemployment claims were 206,000 in the week ended August 29, up 2,000 from the prior week's revised 204,000. The four-week average was 207,250, up 1,500. Continuing claims were 1.779 million in the week ended August 22, up 8,000, and the insured unemployment rate was 1.2%. Weekly claims are subject to revision and seasonal adjustment.

Hires were 5.1 million and total separations were 5.1 million; quits were 3.1 million and layoffs and discharges were 1.7 million. BLS revised June openings down by 177,000 to 7.2 million. These estimates are survey-based and can be revised.

## 4. U.S. growth, activity, productivity, and trade

- The Institute for Supply Management reported September 3 that its August Services PMI rose to 55.4 from 54.1 in July. Business Activity was 61.7, New Orders 60.9, Employment 47.8, Prices 72.6, Backlog of Orders 55.6, and Supplier Deliveries 51.3. ISM said services employment had contracted in 13 of the preceding 18 months and the prices index reached a four-year high. Diffusion indexes above 50 generally indicate expansion except that a Supplier Deliveries reading above 50 indicates slower deliveries.
- ISM reported September 1 that its August Manufacturing PMI was 54.6, down from 55.6 in July but in expansion for an eighth consecutive month. New Orders were 53.7, Production 58.3, Employment 51.2, Supplier Deliveries 59.3, Inventories 50.6, Prices 71.1, and Backlogs 51.8. ISM said five of the six largest manufacturing industries expanded. The survey also reported higher input prices for 23 consecutive months.
- BLS revised second-quarter nonfarm business productivity to a 1.4% annual rate on September 3. Output rose 1.7% and hours worked rose 0.3%; productivity was 2.2% higher than a year earlier. Unit labor costs increased at a 1.2% annual rate and were 1.4% higher year over year. Real hourly compensation fell at a 3.3% annual rate and was down 0.1% from a year earlier. Manufacturing productivity rose at a 2.4% annual rate and manufacturing unit labor costs declined 0.3%. Estimates are revised as source data change.
- BEA's August 26 second estimate put real second-quarter GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate, unchanged at the displayed precision from the advance estimate and below the first quarter's 2.1%. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 4.2%. Real GDI rose 2.2%. The gross domestic purchases price index rose at a 5.8% annual rate; the PCE price index rose 5.3% and core PCE prices rose 3.6% in the quarter. BEA scheduled the third estimate and annual update for September 30.
- BEA and Census reported September 3 that the U.S. goods-and-services trade deficit increased to $88.6 billion in July from a revised $71.2 billion in June. Exports fell $2.1 billion to $310.7 billion and imports rose $15.3 billion to $399.3 billion. The goods deficit was $119.6 billion and the services surplus was $31.0 billion. Capital-goods imports rose $14.4 billion, including computers, accessories, semiconductors, and other equipment. Year to date, the deficit was 29.6% lower than in the same 2025 period, exports were 12.0% higher, and imports were 1.9% higher. Initial trade estimates are revised.
- Census scheduled August wholesale-trade data for September 10, August business-formation statistics for September 11, August retail sales and business inventories for September 16, August housing starts for September 17, August new-home sales for September 24, August durable-goods data for September 25, advance trade/inventory indicators for September 30, August construction spending for October 1, and August factory orders for October 2.
