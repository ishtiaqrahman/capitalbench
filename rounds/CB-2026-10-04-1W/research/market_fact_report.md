## Source ledger

### 1. U.S. labor market

- Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, *The Employment Situation — September 2026*.
- URL: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm
- Released: 2026-10-02 at 08:30 ET.
- Reported facts: nonfarm payroll employment increased by 29,000 in September and the unemployment rate was 4.2%. The labor-force participation rate was 61.8% and the employment-population ratio was 59.2%. Average hourly earnings increased 0.1% over the month and 3.0% over twelve months; the average workweek was 34.4 hours.
- Detail: health-care employment increased 17,000, construction 11,000, and manufacturing 9,000, while financial activities declined 7,000. The one-month private-industry employment diffusion index was 49.0 and the manufacturing diffusion index was 46.5.
- Revisions: July payroll growth was revised from +21,000 to -10,000 and August from +162,000 to +133,000, a combined downward revision of 60,000.
- Uncertainty: September establishment data are preliminary. BLS states that an over-the-month establishment-survey change of roughly 122,000 is needed for statistical significance; survey estimates are subject to sampling error, later reports, and seasonal-factor revisions.

### 2. U.S. manufacturing

- Source: Institute for Supply Management, *ISM PMI Reports Roundup: September Manufacturing*.
- URL: https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/news-publications/inside-supply-management-magazine/blog/2026/2026-10/ism-pmi-reports-roundup-september-2026-manufacturing/
- Released: 2026-10-01.
- Reported facts: the Manufacturing PMI was 54.5, 0.1 point below August and still above the 50 expansion threshold. Production was 56.7, new orders 55.3, backlog of orders 56.4, and employment 52.7.
- Price and sentiment detail: the Prices Index rose 6.8 points to 77.9, its highest level since May. ISM reported that 40% of panel comments were positive and 60% negative; pricing volatility, tariffs, and the Iran conflict were prominent concerns.
- Uncertainty: diffusion indexes describe the breadth and direction of survey responses rather than the magnitude of output changes. ISM also noted a recurring divergence between positive index readings and pessimistic respondent commentary.

### 3. U.S. income, consumption, and inflation

- Source: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, *Personal Income and Outlays, August 2026*.
- URL: https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026
- Released: 2026-09-30 at 08:30 ET.
- Reported facts: personal income increased 0.2% in August, disposable personal income 0.3%, nominal PCE 0.9%, and real PCE 0.6%. The personal saving rate was 4.1%.
- Inflation detail: the PCE price index increased 0.3% over the month and 3.4% from a year earlier. Excluding food and energy, it increased 0.2% over the month and 3.0% from a year earlier.
- Uncertainty: the release incorporated the annual National Economic Accounts update, with revisions beginning in January 2021. Estimates may therefore differ from earlier vintages.

### 4. U.S. growth

- Source: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, *Gross Domestic Product, Second Quarter 2026 — Third Estimate*.
- URL: https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product
- Released: 2026-09-30.
- Reported facts: real GDP increased at a 2.2% annual rate in the second quarter after a revised 2.5% increase in the first quarter. Consumer spending, investment, and exports contributed to the second-quarter increase; imports also increased and subtract from GDP accounting.
- Uncertainty: this is the third quarterly estimate, but later annual revisions can still alter the history.

### 5. Federal Reserve policy and projections

- Sources: Federal Reserve Board, September 16 FOMC statement and Summary of Economic Projections.
- URLs: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm and https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm
- Released: 2026-09-16.
- Reported facts: the FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal-funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00%. The statement characterized activity as expanding at a solid pace and inflation as elevated.
- Projection detail: median 2026 projections were 2.3% real GDP growth, 4.1% unemployment, 3.7% PCE inflation, 3.4% core PCE inflation, and a 4.1% year-end federal-funds rate. Seventeen of eighteen participants judged risks to PCE inflation to be weighted to the upside.
- Uncertainty: projections are individual participants' conditional assessments, not a committee commitment. The next scheduled FOMC meeting is October 27-28 and is not a scheduled SEP meeting.

### 9. Euro-area policy

- Source: European Central Bank, *Monetary policy decisions*.
- URL: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp260910~314e508016.en.html
- Released: 2026-09-10.
