## Prices, income, and policy

- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that the CPI rose 0.1% in July and 3.4% over 12 months, after a 0.4% monthly decline and 3.5% annual increase in June. Core CPI rose 0.2% in July and 2.5% over 12 months.
- July energy prices fell 1.5%, including a nearly 3% decline in gasoline. Grocery prices rose 0.1%, airline fares rose 2.2%, medical care rose 0.4%, and used-car and truck prices rose 0.4%.
- The Producer Price Index for final demand was unchanged in July and rose 4.7% over 12 months, down from 5.5% in June. Core producer prices excluding food and energy rose 0.2% in July and 4.2% over 12 months.
- BEA reported that the June PCE price index fell 0.1% from May and rose 3.7% over 12 months. Core PCE rose 0.1% in June and 3.3% over 12 months.
- June personal income and disposable personal income each rose 0.2%. Nominal personal consumption expenditures rose 0.3%; the saving rate was 2.7%.
- On July 29, the FOMC maintained the federal-funds target range at 3.50%–3.75% by a 9–3 vote. Three members preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The Committee said inflation remained elevated relative to its 2% goal.

## Labor, consumers, and business activity

- Nonfarm payroll employment fell by 23,000 in July, and May and June were revised down by a combined 103,000. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% as labor-force participation fell to 61.4%.
- July local public-school employment fell by 50,000, restaurant and bar employment by 26,000, and retail employment by 19,000. Construction added 22,000 jobs and manufacturing added 5,000.
- Average hourly earnings rose 3.2% from July 2025. The establishment payroll estimate remains subject to revision.
- June job openings were 7.36 million versus 7.54 million in May. Layoffs were little changed at 1.8 million; gross hires rose slightly to 5.3 million.
- Advance retail and food-services sales fell 0.6% in July after a revised 0.2% June gain. Sales excluding gasoline stations and motor-vehicle dealers fell 0.2%; the retail control group fell 0.4%; food-services sales rose 0.5%.
- Retail-sales values are not adjusted for price changes and largely exclude services such as travel and lodging. The advance estimate is subject to revision.
- The preliminary University of Michigan consumer-sentiment index for August was 51.0, down from the final July reading of 55.2. The preliminary result covers the early part of the month and will be revised.
- The ISM Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.6 in July from 53.3. New orders were 56.7, production 58.5, employment 52.8, and prices 71.1.
- The ISM Services PMI was 54.1 in July versus 54.0 in June. Business activity was 59.1, new orders 57.2, employment 47.4, and prices 70.3. ISM figures are diffusion indexes; values above 50 generally denote expansion.

## Output, trade, and housing

- BEA's advance estimate put real Q2 GDP growth at a 1.5% annualized rate after 2.1% in Q1. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose at a 3.9% rate, and consumer spending rose at a 3.2% rate.
- The Q2 gross-domestic-purchases price index rose at a 5.7% annualized rate. The advance GDP estimate is scheduled for revision on August 26.
- Preliminary Q2 nonfarm business productivity rose at a 1.4% annualized rate; unit labor costs rose 1.3%.
- The June goods-and-services trade deficit narrowed to $73.3 billion from a revised $77.6 billion in May. The goods deficit was $102.1 billion and the services surplus was $28.8 billion.
- June housing starts were at a 1.427 million annual rate, 19.0% above revised May. Census reported a ±15.9 percentage-point confidence interval for the monthly change. Single-family starts were 895,000, down 0.2% with a ±10.2-point interval.
- June permits were at a 1.367 million annual rate, down 3.0% from May and 2.3% from a year earlier.
- Existing-home sales fell 1.7% in July to a 4.06 million annual rate and rose 0.7% from a year earlier. The median sales price was $434,100, up 2.0% over 12 months; unsold inventory was 1.54 million homes.
- Freddie Mac's average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 6.67% for the week of August 13, versus 6.69% the prior week and 6.58% a year earlier.
