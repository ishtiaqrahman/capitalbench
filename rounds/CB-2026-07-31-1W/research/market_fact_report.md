## Rates, Policy, Growth, Inflation, and Compensation

- The Federal Open Market Committee voted 9-3 on July 29 to maintain the federal-funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie Logan preferred to raise the range by 25 basis points.
- The FOMC said available indicators suggested solid economic activity, job gains had kept pace with population growth, unemployment had changed little, and inflation remained elevated relative to 2%. It said outlook uncertainty remained elevated and supply shocks including energy were adding to inflation.
- AP reported that the ten-year U.S. Treasury yield rose to 4.71% late July 31 from 4.68% late July 30 and from 3.97% before the Middle East war.
- The Bureau of Economic Analysis advance estimate showed real GDP increased at a 1.5% annual rate in the second quarter after 2.1% in the first quarter. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers increased 3.9% after 1.7%. The advance estimate is subject to revision; the second estimate is scheduled for August 26.
- BEA reported that the gross-domestic-purchases price index increased 5.7%, the PCE price index 5.1%, and core PCE 3.4% in the second quarter, all at annual rates.
- BEA reported June personal income up 0.2%, disposable personal income up 0.2%, nominal PCE up 0.3%, real PCE up 0.4%, and a 2.7% saving rate.
- The June PCE price index fell 0.1% from May and rose 3.7% from June 2025. Core PCE rose 0.1% on the month and 3.3% over twelve months. BEA updated April and May estimates using revised source data.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that civilian compensation costs increased 0.9% in the second quarter. Wages and salaries increased 0.9% and benefit costs increased 1.0%.
- Over the year ended June, civilian compensation increased 3.4%, wages and salaries 3.2%, and benefits 3.8%. Private-industry compensation increased 3.3%; inflation-adjusted private-industry wages and salaries decreased 0.4%. Employment Cost Index estimates are subject to sampling and nonsampling error.

## Labor and Near-Term U.S. Activity

- The U.S. Department of Labor reported 197,000 seasonally adjusted initial unemployment claims for the week ended July 25, up 9,000 from the prior week's revised 188,000. The four-week average fell 5,000 to 202,750.
- Insured unemployment for the week ended July 18 was 1.782 million, down 7,000 from the prior week's revised level. The insured unemployment rate was unchanged at 1.2%.
- BLS reported on July 2 that June nonfarm payroll employment increased by 57,000 and unemployment was 4.2%. Labor-force participation fell 0.3 percentage point to 61.5%. April and May payroll estimates were revised down by a combined 74,000. The July Employment Situation was scheduled for August 7 and its outcome was unknown at the cutoff.
- The Census Bureau's August calendar scheduled June construction spending for August 3; June manufacturers' orders for August 4; June wholesale trade for August 6; July retail sales for August 14; July housing starts for August 18; July new-home sales for August 25; and July durable-goods orders for August 26.

Trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow rose 33% to $161.4 billion, while free cash flow changed to a $7.6 billion outflow, primarily because property and equipment purchases increased by $66.1 billion.

## Energy and Geopolitical Context

- AP reported Brent crude rose 1.2% on July 31 to settle at $87.93 per barrel. The ten-year Treasury yield and crude prices remained well above their pre-war levels.
- On August 2, President Donald Trump said the United States would hold off on new strikes against Iran and claimed parameters existed for an agreement that included reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's government spokesperson said no current discussion to reopen the strait was taking place and that Iran would not return to the pre-February 28 status. No agreement had been reached by the cutoff, and mediation was continuing.
- The International Energy Agency's July report estimated June global oil supply rose 4.1 million barrels per day to 98.8 million, still 9.4 million below the pre-war level. Gulf exports averaged 16.1 million barrels per day versus 24 million before the war.
- The IEA reported global observed oil stocks rose 21 million barrels in June, while onshore inventories fell 96 million and product markets remained tight. It reduced its 2026 demand-growth forecast by 1 million barrels per day and raised its 2027 forecast by 2 million; its projections depended on de-escalation and restored Strait flows.
- On August 2, seven OPEC+ countries decided to implement a 188,000-barrel-per-day production adjustment in September from voluntary adjustments announced in April 2023. They said the next monthly meeting would be September 6.
- The OPEC+ Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee highlighted risks from attacks on energy infrastructure and disruptions to maritime routes, noted overall conformity for participating countries in May and June, and scheduled its next meeting for October 4.

## International Policy and Activity

- The Bank of Japan voted 8-1 on July 31 to keep the uncollateralized overnight call rate around 1.0%. Hajime Takata proposed 1.25%, citing upside price risks.
- The Bank of Japan's July outlook said Japan's economy was expected to grow moderately at a slower rate in fiscal 2026, supported by government measures, accommodative financial conditions, and global AI-related demand while higher crude prices weighed on activity. It projected CPI excluding fresh food would accelerate clearly above 2% from the second half of fiscal 2026 before declining toward around 2% later.
- The European Central Bank kept its July 23 policy rates unchanged at 2.25% on the deposit facility, 2.40% on main refinancing operations, and 2.65% on the marginal lending facility. It said energy-price uncertainty remained high and decisions would remain data-dependent and meeting-by-meeting.
- Eurostat's July flash estimate put euro-area inflation at 2.9%, up from 2.8% in June. Energy inflation increased to 10.0% from 8.5%; services to 3.3% from 3.2%; food, alcohol, and tobacco slowed to 1.2% from 1.5%; and non-energy industrial goods increased to 0.9% from 0.7%. Inflation excluding energy, food, alcohol, and tobacco was 2.5%, up from 2.4%. The flash estimate is preliminary; full July data are scheduled for August 19.
- China's National Bureau of Statistics reported second-quarter real GDP growth of 4.3% year over year and 0.9% quarter over quarter. First-half CPI rose 1.0%, core CPI 1.2%, and producer prices 1.5%. The agency said strong supply and weak demand remained imbalanced and external uncertainty had increased.
- AP reported South Korea's Kospi rose 17.9% on July 31 but remained down 22% for July; Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each rose at least 26.8% that day.
