## July 15 Market Close And Cross-Asset Facts

- The S&P 500 rose 28.81 points, or 0.4%, to 7,572.40; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 150.37 points, or 0.3%, to 52,658.64; the Nasdaq Composite rose 162.22 points, or 0.6%, to 26,269.23; and the Russell 2000 rose 11.50 points, or 0.4%, to 2,976.26.
- For the week through July 15, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq were down less than 0.1%, the Dow was up less than 0.1%, and the Russell 2000 was down 0.1%.
- Year to date through July 15, the S&P 500 was up 10.6%, the Dow was up 9.6%, the Nasdaq was up 13.0%, and the Russell 2000 was up 19.9%.
- The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.55% from 4.58% late July 14 and 4.62% late July 13.
- Brent crude briefly traded above $86 and settled at $84.95 a barrel, up 0.3% for the day.
- South Korea's Kospi rose 6.2%. Hong Kong rose 1.4%, while Shanghai fell 0.3% after China's official statistics agency reported second-quarter GDP growth of 4.3% year over year, down from 5.0% in the first quarter.

## Inflation, Federal Reserve, Growth, And Labor

- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported the Producer Price Index for final demand fell 0.3% in June after rising 0.6% in May and 1.1% in April. The 12-month rate slowed to 5.5% from 6.0% in May.
- Final-demand goods prices fell 1.4%, including a 6.4% decline in energy and a 0.6% decline in food. Gasoline prices fell 12.0%. Final-demand services rose 0.2%.
- Final demand excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.1% in June and 5.1% over 12 months. BLS said some February-through-May PPI figures were revised for late reports and respondent corrections.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported June CPI fell 0.4% month over month and rose 3.5% over 12 months. Core CPI was unchanged in June and rose 2.6% over 12 months.
- The Federal Reserve's July Beige Book said activity increased at a slight to moderate pace in eleven of twelve Districts in late May and June. Consumer spending edged up, with several Districts reporting weaker discretionary spending or trading down as fuel prices rose.
- The Beige Book said manufacturing grew modestly to moderately in most Districts, led by data-center, machinery, and defense orders. Employment rose on balance, while seven Districts reported little or no employment change.
- Prices increased moderately overall and at the same or a slower pace than in the prior report in every District. Energy, transportation, raw-material, tariff, and Middle East conflict costs were cited; several contacts reported greater customer price sensitivity.
- The Beige Book said commercial loan quality was stable, consumer loan quality ticked down, and outlooks generally called for continued expansion with elevated fuel-cost uncertainty. Its information was collected on or before July 6.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported June payroll growth of 57,000, an unemployment rate of 4.2%, a labor-force participation rate of 61.5%, and 3.5% year-over-year average hourly earnings growth.
- The number of long-term unemployed was 1.9 million, up 286,000 from a year earlier. Professional and business services, social assistance, and health care added jobs, while leisure and hospitality employment fell by 61,000.
- The Department of Labor reported initial unemployment claims of 215,000 for the week ended July 4 and a four-week average of 218,750.
- China's National Bureau of Statistics reported first-half GDP growth of 4.7% and second-quarter growth of 4.3%. First-half primary-industry value added rose 3.7%, secondary-industry value added 3.9%, and services value added 5.2%.

## Energy And Strait Of Hormuz Status

- The U.S. military reimposed a blockade on Iranian ports early July 15 after attacks on ships trying to transit the Strait of Hormuz. U.S. Central Command said it conducted another wave of strikes over seven hours.
- Iran's Revolutionary Guard threatened to halt energy exports from the Middle East. Missile alerts were reported in Bahrain and Kuwait as Iran launched fire toward Gulf countries hosting U.S. forces.
- The United States had imposed the blockade in April, lifted it in June after an interim agreement, and reimposed it as talks stalled and fighting around the strait intensified.
- President Donald Trump dropped a previously announced 20% transit fee before the blockade resumed, citing requests from Gulf allies.
- Shipping volumes, transit conditions, military duration, energy flows, negotiations, and further policy actions remained unresolved at the research cutoff.

## Source-Reported Uncertainty And Caveats

- June CPI and PPI both declined month over month, while their year-over-year headline rates remained 3.5% and 5.5%, respectively. June energy-price declines predated the latest increase in oil prices and renewed military activity.
- The Beige Book describes information collected by July 6 rather than conditions at the July 15 close. It is a summary of outside contacts and not a statement of Federal Reserve officials' views.
- The Strait of Hormuz dispute was changing rapidly. The blockade and renewed strikes were reported as active, while shipping volumes, energy flows, negotiations, and the duration of hostilities remained unresolved.
- Corporate reports cover completed periods; published guidance is forward-looking and can differ from actual results. Tesla explicitly cautioned that its operating figures do not determine quarterly financial performance.
- Payroll, producer-price, and other economic estimates are subject to revision. Scheduled corporate and economic releases were not known at the cutoff, and dates can be changed by the publishing organization.
