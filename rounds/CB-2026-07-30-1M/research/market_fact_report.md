## Rates, Policy, Growth, and Inflation

- The Federal Open Market Committee voted 9-3 on July 29 to maintain the federal-funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie Logan preferred to raise the range by 25 basis points.
- The FOMC said available indicators suggested solid economic activity, job gains had kept pace with population growth, unemployment had changed little, and inflation remained elevated relative to 2%. It said outlook uncertainty remained elevated, in part because of the Middle East conflict, and that supply shocks including energy were adding to inflation.
- AP reported that the ten-year U.S. Treasury yield was 4.67% late July 30, unchanged from late July 29, while the thirty-year yield rose to 5.22% from 5.20%.
- The Bureau of Economic Analysis advance estimate showed real GDP increased at a 1.5% annual rate in the second quarter, after 2.1% in the first quarter. Consumer spending, investment, and exports increased; government spending decreased; imports increased.
- BEA reported that real final sales to private domestic purchasers increased 3.9% in the second quarter after 1.7% in the first. The gross-domestic-purchases price index increased 5.7%, the PCE price index 5.1%, and core PCE 3.4%, all at annual rates. The advance GDP estimate is subject to revision; the second estimate is scheduled for August 26.
- BEA reported June personal income up 0.2%, disposable personal income up 0.2%, nominal PCE up 0.3%, and real PCE up 0.4%. The saving rate was 2.7%.
- The June PCE price index fell 0.1% from May and rose 3.7% from June 2025. Core PCE rose 0.1% on the month and 3.3% over twelve months. BEA updated April and May personal-income estimates using revised source data.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on July 14 that June CPI fell 0.4% month over month and rose 3.5% over twelve months. Core CPI was unchanged on the month and rose 2.6% over twelve months. Chained-CPI readings remain subject to revision.
- BLS reported on July 15 that June final-demand PPI fell 0.3% month over month and rose 5.5% over twelve months. Goods prices fell 1.4%, services prices rose 0.2%, and PPI estimates can be revised.

## Labor, Consumption, Manufacturing, and Housing

- The U.S. Department of Labor reported 197,000 seasonally adjusted initial unemployment claims for the week ended July 25, up 9,000 from the prior week's revised 188,000. The four-week average fell 5,000 to 202,750.
- Insured unemployment for the week ended July 18 was 1.782 million, down 7,000 from the prior week's revised level. The insured unemployment rate was unchanged at 1.2%.
- BLS reported on July 2 that June nonfarm payroll employment increased by 57,000 and unemployment was 4.2%. Labor-force participation fell 0.3 percentage point to 61.5%. April and May payroll estimates were revised down by a combined 74,000. Survey estimates are subject to sampling error and later revision.
- AP reported on July 28 that the Conference Board consumer-confidence index fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June. Current labor-market views weakened, while six-month labor-market expectations improved slightly but remained negative.
- The Census Bureau reported June retail and food-services sales of $768.6 billion, up 0.2% from May and 6.7% from June 2025. The monthly estimate's ±0.4 percentage-point sampling margin did not establish that the monthly change differed from zero.
- Census reported June durable-goods orders rose 0.3% after a 4.0% May decline. Orders excluding transportation rose 0.6%, orders excluding defense rose 0.3%, and computers and electronic-product orders rose 3.1%.
- Census and HUD reported June new-home sales at a 628,000 seasonally adjusted annual rate, 1.6% above May and 5.6% below June 2025. Sampling margins were wider than both estimates. Inventory represented 9.3 months of supply and the median sales price was $398,300.

## Company Results Available at the Cutoff

- Microsoft reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $90.0 billion, up 18% year over year; Microsoft Cloud revenue of $59.3 billion, up 27%; Azure and other cloud-services revenue growth of 43%; and commercial remaining performance obligation of $678 billion, up 84%.
- Microsoft reported More Personal Computing revenue down 4%, Windows OEM and devices revenue down 7%, and Xbox content and services revenue down 10%. Its GAAP results included an Anthropic investment gain, value-realignment-program costs, severance, and an Xbox content-asset impairment.
- Meta reported second-quarter revenue of $60.801 billion, up 28%, while costs and expenses rose 55%. Operating income fell 8%, net income fell 14%, and diluted EPS fell 13%. Results included legal and severance charges.
- Meta forecast third-quarter revenue of $61 billion to $64 billion, full-year expenses of $165 billion to $169 billion, and full-year capital expenditures of $130 billion to $145 billion. These are company forecasts, not observed outcomes.
- Apple reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $109.4 billion, up 16% year over year, gross margin of 50.1%, and diluted EPS of $2.02, up 29%. Apple said tariff refunds added approximately 2 percentage points to gross margin and $0.11 to EPS.
- Apple reported June-quarter revenue records for the company, iPhone, Mac, and Services, and said its installed base reached a record across major product categories and geographic segments. Apple identified economic, trade, geopolitical, supply-chain, technology, legal, and regulatory uncertainties.
- Amazon reported second-quarter net sales of $200.6 billion, up 20% year over year; operating income of $27.5 billion, up from $19.2 billion; and AWS sales of $42.2 billion, up 37%. AWS operating income was $16.6 billion, up from $10.2 billion.
- Amazon reported net income of $62.6 billion, including $53.4 billion of non-operating pretax income primarily from Anthropic investments. Trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow rose 33% to $161.4 billion, while free cash flow changed to a $7.6 billion outflow, primarily because property and equipment purchases increased by $66.1 billion.
- Amazon forecast third-quarter net sales of $197 billion to $202 billion, 9% to 12% growth, and operating income of $22.5 billion to $26.5 billion. These company forecasts are subject to substantial uncertainty, including energy, foreign-exchange, trade, supply, demand, inflation, interest-rate, investment, and geopolitical risks.

## Cross-Asset and International Context

- AP reported that Brent crude fell 1.4% on July 30 to settle at $86.88 per barrel. AP reported that Brent had traded near $72 early in July and above $102 in the prior week amid the Middle East conflict and uncertainty over tanker access.
- AP reported that South Korea's Kospi fell 1.2% on July 30 and had fallen 34% during July after more than doubling in the first half of 2026. France's CAC 40 rose 0.9%. Samsung Electronics fell 0.7% after reporting record spring profit but results below analysts' expectations.
- The Bank of Japan voted 8-1 on July 31 to keep the uncollateralized overnight call rate around 1.0%. Hajime Takata proposed 1.25%, citing upside price risks from overseas demand shocks and changes in overseas financial conditions.
- The Bank of Japan's July outlook said Japan's economy was expected to grow moderately at a slower rate in fiscal 2026, supported by government measures, accommodative financial conditions, and global AI-related demand while higher crude prices weighed on activity.
- The Bank of Japan projected CPI excluding fresh food would accelerate clearly above 2% from the second half of fiscal 2026, then decline toward around 2% later in the projection period. It described economic risks as generally balanced and inflation risks as skewed upward, with particular uncertainty from the Middle East, AI-related demand, and foreign-exchange developments.
- The European Central Bank kept its policy rates unchanged July 23: 2.25% on the deposit facility, 2.40% on main refinancing operations, and 2.65% on the marginal lending facility. It said energy-price uncertainty remained high and decisions would remain meeting-by-meeting.
- Eurostat reported final June euro-area inflation of 2.8%, down from 3.2% in May. Eurostat's July flash estimate was scheduled after the research cutoff on July 31 and was unavailable for this report.
- China's National Bureau of Statistics reported second-quarter real GDP growth of 4.3% year over year and 0.9% quarter over quarter. First-half manufacturing value added rose 5.6%, high-technology manufacturing rose 13.3%, fixed-asset investment fell 5.7%, and real-estate development investment fell 18.0%.
