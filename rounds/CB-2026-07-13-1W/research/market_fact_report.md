## July 13 Market Close And Breadth

- The S&P 500 fell 60.05 points, or 0.8%, to 7,515.34; the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 138.37 points, or 0.3%, to 52,498.64; the Nasdaq Composite fell 408.43 points, or 1.6%, to 25,873.18; and the Russell 2000 fell 24.64 points, or 0.8%, to 2,953.17.
- Year to date through July 13, the S&P 500 was up 9.8%, the Dow was up 9.2%, the Nasdaq was up 11.3%, and the Russell 2000 was up 19.0%.
- Micron Technology fell 4.4%, Nvidia fell 3.5%, and SK Hynix's U.S.-traded receipts fell 9.3%. TSMC shares rose 1.0% in Taiwan and fell 2.9% in U.S. trading.
- South Korea's Kospi fell 8.9%, Shanghai fell 2.1%, and Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.9%. AP described European index moves as modest.
- The close table shows the Nasdaq's decline was twice the percentage decline of the S&P 500 and Russell 2000. These index comparisons are descriptive breadth context, not a forecast.

## Rates, Energy, And Inflation Context

- Associated Press reported Brent crude rose 9.6% to $83.30 a barrel on July 13, while remaining below its wartime peak near $120.
- The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.61% from 4.56% late July 10 and from 3.97% before the war with Iran began.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported May CPI rose 0.5% month over month and 4.2% over 12 months. Core CPI rose 0.2% in May and 2.9% over 12 months.
- The June CPI report is scheduled for July 14 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. It was not published at the research cutoff.
- Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said July 13 that there remained a credible case for inflation to move toward 2% at the current policy setting, while also saying persistent or rising inflation could require tighter policy in the near term.
- Waller said a further high core-inflation reading would require the FOMC to consider tightening in the near term, and that several months of lower readings would be needed for confidence that inflation was moving in the intended direction.

## Labor And Growth Baseline

- The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported June nonfarm payrolls changed by 57,000 and the unemployment rate was 4.2%.
- The labor-force participation rate fell 0.3 percentage point to 61.5%, and the employment-population ratio fell 0.2 percentage point to 59.0%.
- Average hourly earnings rose 0.3% in June and 3.5% over 12 months.
- April payroll growth was revised down by 31,000 to 148,000 and May was revised down by 43,000 to 129,000, a combined downward revision of 74,000.
- June employment rose in professional and business services, social assistance, and health care, while leisure and hospitality employment fell by 61,000.

## Energy And Geopolitical Datapoints

- Associated Press reported that the United States and Iran each asserted control over the Strait of Hormuz on July 13 after renewed exchanges of fire.
- President Donald Trump announced that the United States would reinstate a blockade on Iranian shipping and seek payments equal to 20% of cargo value from other ships for U.S.-provided passage protection.
- The U.S. military said the blockade of Iranian ports would resume July 14 at 4:00 p.m. Eastern.
- Iran said it retained the right to manage traffic through the strait and opposed U.S. interference. The International Maritime Organization said it remained opposed to mandatory transit tolls in international waterways.
- AP reported fighting had kept oil tankers from using the strait and that the interim peace arrangement remained under strain. Control, transit volumes, duration, legal status, and energy-price effects were unresolved at the cutoff.

## Source-Reported Uncertainty And Caveats

- The July 13 close combined lower U.S. equity indexes, larger declines in the Nasdaq and several chip shares, higher oil prices, and a higher 10-year Treasury yield.
- The Strait of Hormuz dispute was changing rapidly. Announced blockade timing and transit terms were future statuses at the cutoff, not completed events.
- Waller described two credible inflation paths and made any near-term tightening conditional on incoming data. His remarks are one policymaker's view, not an FOMC decision.
- The June CPI, bank earnings, TSMC results, and all other scheduled releases were not known at the cutoff. Forecasts and company guidance are labeled as such.
- TSMC's monthly revenue figures are unaudited. Tesla explicitly cautioned that production and delivery figures alone do not determine quarterly financial performance.
- Payroll estimates are subject to revision; April and May were revised down by a combined 74,000 in the June report.
- The generated full-universe price context is a mechanical artifact. It is sorted by option order, not performance, and price history is descriptive context rather than a forecast.
