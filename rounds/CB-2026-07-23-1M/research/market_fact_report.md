### Spending, production, housing, and GDP

The Census Bureau's July 16 advance retail report estimated June U.S. retail and food-services sales at $768.6 billion, up 0.2% from May and 6.7% from June 2025. Census published a ±0.4 percentage-point sampling interval for the monthly change and ±0.5 point for the annual change. BEA revised the Q1 rate up 0.5 percentage point from its second estimate, primarily because imports were revised down, partly offset by a downward revision to consumer spending.

## Monetary policy status at the cutoff

The Federal Open Market Committee voted 12–0 on June 17 to maintain the federal-funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. The July 28–29 meeting had not occurred by the research cutoff.

The European Central Bank announced on July 23 that it left its three key rates unchanged: the deposit-facility rate at 2.25%, the main-refinancing-operations rate at 2.40%, and the marginal-lending-facility rate at 2.65%. The ECB stated that energy prices remained highly volatile, were close to the June baseline but well above pre-conflict levels, and that the full inflation effect had yet to pass through. It reiterated a meeting-by-meeting, data-dependent approach without pre-committing to a rate path. APP and PEPP portfolios continued to decline because principal payments were not being reinvested.

The Bank of England had maintained Bank Rate at 3.75% by a 7–2 vote at its June meeting. Its next policy announcement and Monetary Policy Report were scheduled for July 30.
