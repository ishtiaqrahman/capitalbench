## U.S. policy, growth, inflation, and labor facts

- On July 29, the Federal Open Market Committee maintained the federal-funds target at 3.50%-3.75% by a 9-3 vote. Three voters preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The statement said economic activity was expanding at a solid pace, job gains had kept pace with the workforce, unemployment had changed little, and inflation remained elevated relative to 2%.
- BEA's advance estimate put real Q2 2026 GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate after 2.1% in Q1. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers increased at a 3.9% annual rate. The gross-domestic-purchases price index rose 5.7%; headline and core PCE prices rose at annual rates of 5.1% and 3.4%. These are advance estimates.
- In June, personal income and disposable personal income each rose 0.2%, current-dollar PCE rose 0.3%, and real PCE rose 0.4%. The saving rate was 2.7%. The PCE price index fell 0.1% month over month and rose 3.7% year over year; core PCE rose 0.1% month over month and 3.3% year over year.
- June CPI fell 0.4% month over month and rose 3.5% year over year. Core CPI was unchanged on the month and rose 2.6% year over year. Energy fell 5.7% in June but remained 15.7% higher than a year earlier. The July CPI release is scheduled for August 12, after the weekly exit and inside the monthly window.
- June final-demand PPI fell 0.3% month over month and rose 5.5% year over year; final demand less food, energy, and trade services rose 0.1% on the month and 3.0% over the year. June figures are preliminary.
- June payroll employment increased by 57,000 and unemployment was 4.2%; BLS described both as little changed. July employment is scheduled for August 7, inside both scoring windows.
- June job openings were little changed at 7.4 million (4.4% rate). Hires were 5.3 million, separations 5.4 million, quits 3.2 million, and layoffs/discharges 1.8 million.

## Business, production, consumer, housing, and trade facts

- ISM's July Manufacturing PMI was 55.6, up from 53.3, marking a seventh month of expansion and the highest reading since May 2022. New orders were 56.7, production 58.5, employment 52.8, supplier deliveries 58.9, and prices 71.1. Fifteen industries reported growth and one reported contraction.
- June industrial production rose 0.1% and was 1.1% above June 2025. Manufacturing output was unchanged; capacity utilization was 76.1%, 3.3 percentage points below its 1972-2025 average. June data were preliminary.
- June retail and food-services sales were $768.6 billion, up 0.2% month over month and 6.7% year over year. Census stated the monthly change was not statistically distinguishable from zero at the 90% confidence level. July retail sales are scheduled for August 14 inside the monthly window.
- July Consumer Confidence was 90.8, down 1.4 points. The Present Situation Index fell to 114.9 and the Expectations Index was unchanged at 74.7. The survey cutoff was July 22; the publisher noted that later conflict escalation was not fully reflected.
- June housing starts were at a 1.427 million annual rate, up 19.0% from May; single-family starts were 895,000, down 0.2%. Permits were 1.367 million, down 3.0% from May. Census confidence intervals were wide.
- The June goods-and-services trade deficit was $73.3 billion, down $4.4 billion from the revised May level. Exports fell $2.9 billion to $314.7 billion and imports fell $7.3 billion to $388.0 billion. Year to date, the deficit was 33.8% below the comparable 2025 period; exports were up 11.7% and imports up 0.4%.
