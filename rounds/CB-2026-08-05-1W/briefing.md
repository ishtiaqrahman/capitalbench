## Market close, rates, and Treasury financing

- On August 5 the S&P 500 closed at 7,723.55, down 0.2%; the Dow closed at a record 54,349.12, up 0.5%; and the Nasdaq Composite closed at 26,363.44, down 0.8%.
- Brent crude was reported at $79.45 a barrel, down 0.1% that day. It had reached $102 during the conflict.
- Treasury's August 5 par yields were 4.03% at one year, 4.18% at two years, 4.33% at five years, 4.63% at ten years, and 5.17% at thirty years. Treasury describes these as interpolated indicative yields observed near 3:30 p.m. ET.

## U.S. policy, growth, inflation, and labor

- On July 29 the FOMC maintained the federal-funds target at 3.50%-3.75% by a 9-3 vote. Three members preferred a 25-basis-point increase. The statement said inflation remained elevated relative to 2%.
- The advance estimate of annualized Q2 real GDP growth was 1.5%. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 3.9%. The gross-domestic-purchases price index increased 5.7%; headline and core PCE prices rose at 5.1% and 3.4% annual rates. A second estimate is scheduled for August 26.
- In June, personal income and disposable income each rose 0.2%, nominal spending rose 0.3%, real spending rose 0.4%, and the saving rate was 2.7%. Headline PCE prices fell 0.1% on the month and rose 3.7% over the year; core PCE rose 0.1% and 3.3%.
- June CPI fell 0.4% from May and rose 3.5% over twelve months. Core CPI was unchanged on the month and rose 2.6% over the year.
- June final-demand PPI fell 0.3% from May and rose 5.5% over twelve months. Goods prices declined 1.4% and services prices rose 0.2%. June data are preliminary.
- BLS reported 57,000 additional payroll jobs in June and unemployment at 4.2%, describing both as little changed.
- June job openings were little changed at 7.4 million. Hires were 5.3 million, total separations 5.4 million, quits 3.2 million, and layoffs and discharges 1.8 million.
- ADP estimated that private employers added 44,000 jobs in July, down from 95,000 in June. Reported year-over-year pay growth was 4.4% for job stayers and 7.0% for job changers. ADP is separate from the BLS payroll report.

## Business activity, consumers, housing, and trade

- The July ISM Manufacturing PMI was 55.6, up from 53.3 and the highest since May 2022. New orders were 56.7 and production was 58.5.
- The July ISM Services PMI was 54.1, compared with 54.0 in June. Business activity was 59.1, new orders 57.2, employment 47.4, and prices 70.3.
- June industrial production increased 0.1% and was 1.1% above a year earlier. Manufacturing output was unchanged. Capacity utilization was 76.1%, 3.3 percentage points below its 1972-2025 average. June data are preliminary.
- Advance June retail and food-services sales were $768.6 billion, up 0.2% on the month and 6.7% on the year. The monthly change was not statistically different from zero at 90% confidence, and the values are not price-adjusted.
- June housing starts were at a 1.427 million annual rate, 19.0% above revised May. Single-family starts were 895,000, down 0.2%. Permits were at a 1.367 million rate, down 3.0%. Sampling intervals were wide.
- July Consumer Confidence declined 1.4 points to 90.8. The Present Situation Index was 114.9 and the Expectations Index was 74.7. The preliminary online survey ended July 22.
- The June goods-and-services trade deficit narrowed to $73.3 billion. Exports were $314.7 billion and imports were $388.0 billion. The first-half deficit was 33.8% below the same 2025 period.

## Global, energy, geopolitical, and corporate facts

- The IMF's July forecast put global growth at 3.0% in 2026 and 3.4% in 2027 and stated that global disinflation had stalled. These values are forecasts.
- On July 23 the ECB kept its deposit, main-refinancing, and marginal-lending rates at 2.25%, 2.40%, and 2.65%. It said the full inflationary effect of the energy shock had not yet played out.
- The IEA reported June global oil supply at 98.8 million barrels per day, 9.4 million below prewar levels. Gulf exports including bypass routes were 16.1 million barrels per day, compared with a 24 million prewar average. The outlook depends on de-escalation and recovering tanker traffic.
- On August 5, Iranian and Omani negotiators were reported to have completed a draft Strait of Hormuz arrangement that still lacked final Iranian approval. Port-blockade relief, control, and service-fee terms remained disputed.
- About three quarters of S&P 500 companies had reported. Wall Street's aggregate profit-growth estimate for the completed season was reported at 50%; that estimate does not describe every company.
- On August 5, Disney rose 3.6%, Booking Holdings rose 6.6%, SpaceX fell 13.6%, Nvidia rose 3.4%, and AMD fell 7% following company-specific earnings or supplier news.
